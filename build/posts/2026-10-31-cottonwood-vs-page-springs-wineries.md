Choosing between Cottonwood vs Page Springs wineries comes down to one question: do you want to taste among the vines, or walk from tasting room to tasting room? Page Springs is the vineyard experience, a handful of estate wineries along Oak Creek about 20 to 30 minutes from Sedona. Old Town Cottonwood is the town experience, a cluster of tasting rooms on a few walkable blocks of Main Street, with dinner a few doors away. They sit only 15 to 20 minutes apart, so if you have a full day, the honest answer is to do both.

We have run guests through both since 2004, and we hear this question nearly every day.

## Cottonwood vs Page Springs wineries at a glance

**Page Springs (Cornville)**

- **The feel:** Estate vineyards in a green, sycamore-lined creek bottom. Patios over the water, vines out the window.
- **Getting there:** Take State Route 89A southwest from West Sedona and turn onto Page Springs Road. Plan on 20 to 30 minutes each way.
- **Between stops:** Three wineries sit side by side on Page Springs Road, but you still drive (or get driven) to reach the corridor and to reach D.A. Ranch and Cove Mesa.
- **Best for:** First-timers who want to see where Arizona wine is grown, couples, photographers, and anyone who wants a long, quiet lunch outdoors.

**Old Town Cottonwood**

- **The feel:** A historic 1920s Main Street with covered sidewalks, galleries, shops and restaurants. No vineyards in sight, but wines from around the state.
- **Getting there:** About 19 miles from Uptown Sedona on 89A. Plan on 30 to 40 minutes depending on where you are staying and traffic.
- **Between stops:** Park once and walk. That is the whole appeal.
- **Best for:** Groups who want variety, people who like to wander and shop between pours, and anyone who wants to roll a tasting afternoon straight into dinner.

## Page Springs: tasting where the grapes grow

Page Springs is where we send people who have never had Arizona wine, because the setting explains the wine. The vineyards sit in the high desert along Oak Creek, with warm days and cool nights, and you taste looking out at the place the grapes came from. Our longer [Page Springs wine tasting guide](/blog/page-springs-wine-tasting) goes winery by winery. Here is the short version, with hours checked this week:

- **Page Springs Cellars**, 1500 N. Page Springs Road, is open 365 days a year, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday through Wednesday and 11 a.m. to 8 p.m. Thursday through Saturday, according to its [FAQ page](https://pagespringscellars.com/faqs/). Tastings are 90-minute reservations booked through Tock, and parties of more than six should give advance notice. Outside food and drink are not allowed, but there is a picnic window.
- **Oak Creek Vineyards & Winery**, 1555 N. Page Springs Road, is listed by [Visit Arizona](https://visitarizona.com/directory/oak-creek-vineyards-winery) as open daily, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.
- **Javelina Leap Vineyard**, 1565 N. Page Springs Road, is listed as open daily, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m., by [Visit Arizona](https://www.visitarizona.com/directory/javelina-leap-vineyard), and is known for its estate Zinfandel.
- **D.A. Ranch**, 1901 Dancing Apache Road, is a cattle ranch turned vineyard with springs, a pond and resident goats and donkeys. [Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/directory/da-ranch) lists it as generally open Wednesday through Sunday, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m., and it can close for weddings and private events.
- **Cove Mesa Vineyard**, just over the hill on Western Drive in Cornville, is the newest tasting room in the neighborhood, and the owners are often the ones pouring. Please check the current hours and guest policies with the winery before you go.

**The trade-offs.** Most vineyard rooms close at 6 p.m., so Page Springs is a daytime plan. Page Springs Road is two lanes with curves as it drops to the creek, and lots fill on fall weekends. And someone has to drive, which matters more here than in Cottonwood.

**Dogs.** Page Springs is the friendlier side of the valley for dogs. At Page Springs Cellars, leashed dogs are welcome on the outdoor grounds and patios but not inside the tasting room (service dogs are welcome inside). Other wineries set their own rules, so check before you bring your pup.

## Old Town Cottonwood: a walkable Main Street of tasting rooms

Old Town Cottonwood is the easiest wine afternoon in northern Arizona. The tasting rooms sit a few doors apart on North Main Street, mixed in with antique shops, galleries and restaurants. Our [Old Town Cottonwood wine tasting guide](/blog/old-town-cottonwood-wine-tasting) walks it block by block. The rooms we lean on most:

- **Arizona Stronghold Vineyards**, 1023 N. Main Street, one of the state's larger producers, with fruit grown in southern Arizona. Its [tasting room page](https://azstronghold.com/Tasting-Room/Old-Town-Cottonwood) lists hours of noon to 6 p.m. Sunday through Tuesday, noon to 7 p.m. Wednesday and Thursday, and noon to 8 p.m. Friday and Saturday. Seating is first come, first served; parties of eight or more should call ahead, and the room cannot seat parties larger than ten together.
- **Rubrix Wines** (formerly Burning Tree Cellars), 1040 N. Main Street, a small-batch label at the north end of the street.
- **The Eureka Room**, 1012 N. Main Street, part gallery and part tasting room.
- **Tantrum Wines**, 918 N. Main Street, one of the few female-owned wineries in Arizona, with a whimsical room to match.
- **Da Vines Vineyard Wines & Bistro**, 705 N. Main Street, at the south end, a natural lunch or dinner stop.
- **Merkin Vineyards Hilltop Winery & Trattoria**, 770 N. Verde Heights Drive, a short drive up the hill from Old Town. [Arizona Highways](https://www.arizonahighways.com/business/merkin-vineyards-hilltop-winery-trattoria) notes it is open daily, with pizzas and pastas from a menu that changes often, a greenhouse supplying the kitchen, a production winery you can see into, and a 270-degree view of the valley. Reservations are recommended for eight or more.

The [Cottonwood Chamber of Commerce's wine tasting directory](https://business.cottonwoodchamberaz.org/list/category/wine-tasting-131) is a good cross-check, since the Main Street lineup shifts a little every year.

**The trade-offs.** You do not see vineyards in Old Town, and many of the wines are made from grapes grown in southern Arizona rather than on site. Saturday afternoons get busy and street parking fills early; head for the free lots just off Main. Hours run later than in Page Springs, especially on Friday and Saturday, which makes Cottonwood the better choice for an evening.

## Food, crowds and timing: how they compare

**Food.** Page Springs Cellars has a bistro and Oak Creek Vineyards serves cheeses and snacks, but Page Springs is mostly a lunch plan. Cottonwood wins on food: lunch, dinner and dessert are all within a few blocks, and Merkin's trattoria is minutes away.

**Time of day.** Page Springs is best from late morning through mid-afternoon, when the light is good on the creek and the rooms are all open. Cottonwood is best mid-afternoon into the evening.

**Day of the week.** Both are busy on fall Saturdays. Page Springs Cellars pours every day of the year, and its neighbors on the road are listed as open daily, so a Monday in Page Springs works well. D.A. Ranch is closed early in the week. In Cottonwood, Monday and Tuesday have the shortest hours. Our guide to [wineries open on Sunday and Monday](/blog/wineries-open-sunday-monday-sedona) has the full day-by-day list.

**Season.** Late October and November are prime for both: harvest is wrapping up and the sycamores and cottonwoods along Oak Creek and the Verde River turn gold. Cottonwood sits around 3,300 feet and runs a few degrees warmer than Sedona, which keeps its patios comfortable deep into fall. Old Town's biggest day is Walkin' on Main, a free street festival with a wine garden, classic cars and live music, held on a Saturday in November around Veterans Day; in 2025 it ran 11 a.m. to 5 p.m., per the [Cottonwood Chamber](https://business.cottonwoodchamberaz.org/events/details/19th-annual-walkin-on-main-32391). Check the organizers' site for the 2026 date before you plan around it, and arrive early if you do.

## Why not both? A one-day plan for Page Springs and Cottonwood

The two areas are close enough that a full day covers both without rushing. This is the route our guides use most:

- **11 a.m.:** First tasting at a Page Springs estate, while the patios are quiet.
- **12:15 p.m.:** Second winery on the same road, or lunch at the Page Springs Cellars bistro.
- **2 p.m.:** Drive 15 to 20 minutes to Old Town Cottonwood.
- **2:30 to 5 p.m.:** One or two tasting rooms on Main Street on foot, with shopping in between.
- **5:30 p.m.:** Dinner on Main Street or up the hill at Merkin.

Keep it to four tastings total and share flights when you can. For how Camp Verde, Clarkdale and Jerome fit around this route, see our guide to [Sedona wineries and vineyards](/sedona-wineries-and-vineyards).

**What to book.**

- A Tock reservation at Page Springs Cellars for fall weekends.
- A dinner table in Cottonwood, especially on Friday and Saturday.
- A call ahead anywhere your group is eight or more.

**What to bring.** Photo ID for everyone tasting, water, a light layer for creekside patios once the sun drops behind the canyon walls, comfortable shoes for Main Street, and a cooler bag so bottles do not bake in the car.

**When to go.** Wednesday through Friday is our sweet spot for either area. Start Page Springs by late morning, save Cottonwood for the afternoon and evening, and check the current hours of every stop the day before, since private events and seasonal changes close rooms with little notice.

## The bottom line

Pick Page Springs if you want vineyards, creekside patios and the story of Arizona wine at its source; pick Old Town Cottonwood if you want to walk, shop, taste widely and stay for dinner; and if you have the day, do Page Springs first and Cottonwood second.
