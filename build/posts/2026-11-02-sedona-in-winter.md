Sedona in winter is one of the best-kept secrets in Arizona: afternoons usually reach the high 50s under clear blue skies, the trails and tasting rooms are the quietest they will be all year, and an occasional dusting of snow on the red rocks makes for the most striking photos you will ever take here. Pack layers for cold mornings, plan your outdoor time between about 10 a.m. and 3 p.m., and you will find plenty to do from December through February.

We have guided guests through Sedona and the Verde Valley since 2004, and many of our favorite days on the road happen in winter. Here is what the season actually looks like and what we tell friends to do with it.

## Sedona winter weather: what to expect

Sedona sits around 4,500 feet, high enough for real winter but low enough to stay mild. As [Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/things-to-do/itineraries/sedona-winter-itinerary/) puts it, snow "occasionally dusts the upper reaches of the surrounding mesas and mountains." In town it rarely sticks for long; most snowfalls we see are gone from the roads by early afternoon.

Here are Sedona's average highs and lows, from NOAA's 1991–2020 climate normals as compiled by [Current Results](https://currentresults.com/Weather/Arizona/Places/sedona-temperatures-by-month-average.php):

- **November:** 67 / 41
- **December:** 58 / 33
- **January:** 58 / 34
- **March:** 68 / 40

A few patterns we have seen year after year:

- **The daily swing is big.** A 25-degree jump from breakfast to lunch is normal, so a morning that needs a puffy jacket often turns into a T-shirt afternoon in the sun.
- **Sun matters more than air temperature.** A south-facing patio at 55 degrees feels warm; a shady canyon at the same temperature feels cold.
- **Days are short.** Sunset comes around 5:20 p.m. in December, so start early and plan your evenings indoors or under the stars.
- **Flagstaff is a different world.** It sits about 2,500 feet higher and gets real snow. If a storm is coming, check road conditions before driving north.

## Things to do in Sedona in winter: our 12 favorites

### 1. Hike in the warm part of the day

Winter is prime hiking season for anyone who wilts in the summer heat. Sunny, exposed trails in West Sedona and the Village of Oak Creek are pleasant at midday when shaded canyons are still icy. Start after 10 a.m., carry traction devices if there has been recent snow, and turn back early; the light fades fast. Many trailheads need a Red Rock Pass, which we explain in our [Red Rock Pass guide](/blog/red-rock-pass-sedona).

### 2. Catch snow on the red rocks

The day after a winter storm is the most photographed morning of the year in Sedona. Red sandstone with a white frosting, a deep blue sky and clouds lifting off the buttes is worth setting an alarm for. Get out early, because the sun melts the lower rocks by midday. Drive carefully; roads can be icy in the shade.

### 3. See the holiday lights at Tlaquepaque

Tlaquepaque Arts and Shopping Village kicks off the season with its Christmas tree lighting on Friday, December 4, 2026, from 5 to 6:30 p.m., according to [Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/events-calendar/tlaquepaque-christmas-tree-lighting/). Later in December, Tlaquepaque traditionally hosts its Festival of Lights, when the courtyards glow with luminarias; check the village's calendar for this year's date. It is the most festive evening in town, and it gets crowded, so arrive early.

### 4. Taste wine without the crowds

This is our favorite reason to come in winter. Verde Valley tasting rooms are quiet on weekdays, winemakers have time to talk, and big reds suit a cold afternoon. Many wineries pour year-round; Page Springs Cellars, for example, says on its [FAQ page](https://pagespringscellars.com/faqs/) that it is open 365 days a year. Smaller rooms may shorten winter hours, so check the current hours before you go. Our [wineries and vineyards guide](/sedona-wineries-and-vineyards) and our post on the [best time for wine tasting in Sedona](/blog/best-time-for-wine-tasting-sedona) cover where to go.

### 5. Wander Old Town Cottonwood and Jerome

When the wind picks up, a walkable town beats a trail. Old Town Cottonwood's Main Street packs tasting rooms, restaurants and shops into a few blocks. Up the hill, Jerome's galleries, mining history and tasting rooms are all a short walk apart, and the views across the Verde Valley are often clearest on a crisp winter day. The road up to Jerome is steep and winding, so watch the forecast for ice.

### 6. Visit the Verde Valley's ancient sites

Winter is a lovely time for Montezuma Castle National Monument, a Sinagua cliff dwelling seen from a short, flat, paved loop. The National Park Service lists a $10 adult pass that also covers Tuzigoot National Monument, with kids under 16 free; see the [NPS basic information page](https://home.nps.gov/moca/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm) for current hours, and expect holiday closures. Pair it with a winery in Camp Verde or Cornville and you have an easy, low-effort day.

### 7. Go skiing or play in the snow near Flagstaff

Arizona Snowbowl, just outside Flagstaff, is scheduled to open its 2026–27 season on November 20, 2026, weather permitting, according to [Snowbowl](https://www.snowbowl.ski/100-days-until-scheduled-opening-day/). A ski morning followed by a wine afternoon in the Verde Valley is a very Arizona kind of day. Sedona to Flagstaff takes about 45 to 50 minutes in good conditions; add time for the drive up Snowbowl Road, and check road conditions on AZ 511 before you go.

### 8. See the Grand Canyon in snow

The South Rim is open year-round, and the [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/winter-visits.htm) notes that its roads are drivable except in severe weather. The North Rim is closed to vehicles from December 1 to May 14. The park uses cinders, not salt, on its roads, and recommends over-the-shoe traction devices for icy rim paths. Snow on the canyon walls with thin winter crowds is unforgettable. Our [Sedona to Grand Canyon day trip guide](/blog/sedona-to-grand-canyon-day-trip) has routes and drive times.

### 9. Stargaze on long, clear nights

Winter nights are long, dry and often crystal clear, which makes them some of the best of the year for stargazing. Bundle up more than you think you need; it can drop below freezing soon after dark. Our [Sedona stargazing guide](/blog/sedona-stargazing) lists where to go. In Flagstaff, Lowell Observatory runs evening programs; check [current hours and tickets](https://lowell.edu/visit/) before driving up.

### 10. Book a spa day

Sedona's resort spas are at their best when it is cold outside. A midweek treatment in January or February is easier to book than in spring, and many resorts pair spa time with fireside lounges and red-rock views.

### 11. Catch the Sedona International Film Festival

The festival returns February 20–28, 2027, according to the [Sedona International Film Festival](https://sedonafilmfestival.com/?p=22354). It fills screens around town for nine days and brings a lively, friendly crowd. Book lodging early for that week.

### 12. Shop the galleries

Sedona's art scene is year-round, and winter is when gallery owners have the most time to talk about the work. Tlaquepaque, Uptown and the galleries along State Route 179 are all easy to browse on a cold afternoon.

## Sedona in December, January and February: month by month

**December.** Holiday lights, the Tlaquepaque tree lighting and festive dinners. The week between Christmas and New Year's is one of the busiest of the year, so book lodging and dinner reservations well ahead. The first two weeks of December are wonderfully calm by comparison.

**January.** The quietest month of the year after New Year's weekend. Expect the coldest mornings, the best lodging rates and the easiest restaurant reservations. A snowstorm or two is likely somewhere in northern Arizona.

**February.** Days lengthen, the film festival arrives late in the month and Presidents' Day weekend brings a burst of visitors. By late February, warm afternoons start hinting at spring.

## What to book, what to bring, when to go

**When to go.** For quiet and value, the first two weeks of December or anytime in January after New Year's weekend. For festive energy, the first weekend of December. For culture, the last week of February.

**What to book.**

- Lodging early for Christmas week, New Year's and Presidents' Day weekend.
- Dinner reservations on holiday weekends; many favorite restaurants fill up.
- A driver for wine days. Short days, early darkness and occasional ice make winter the worst time to drive yourself between tasting rooms. [Wine Tours of Sedona](/wine-tours-of-sedona) runs private, all-inclusive tours with tastings and Sedona pickup, and well-behaved dogs are welcome; reach Wine Tours of Sedona at (928) 224-2991 or www.winetoursofsedona.com. For a holiday girls' trip or a big group, SIP Sedona runs small-group wine tours and group transportation; reach SIP Sedona at (928) 308-5166 or www.sipsedona.com.

**What to bring.**

- Layers: a warm jacket, hat and gloves for mornings and evenings, and a light layer for afternoon sun.
- Traction devices for trails after a storm.
- Sunglasses and sunscreen; the winter sun at 4,500 feet is strong.
- Shoes with good grip for icy sidewalks and trailheads.
- Photo ID for wine tasting.
- A flexible plan. If a storm closes roads north, swap Flagstaff for the Verde Valley, which usually stays clear.

**Before you drive.** Check AZ 511 for road conditions, especially for anything heading to Flagstaff or the Grand Canyon. The National Park Service also lists a Grand Canyon roads line at 928-638-7496.

## Is Sedona worth visiting in winter?

Yes, and for some travelers it is the best season of all. You trade summer's heat and fall's crowds for cold mornings, warm afternoons, easy reservations and the chance of snow on the red rocks. If you want long hiking days and green vineyards, come in spring or fall. If you want quiet trails, unhurried tastings and a fireplace at the end of the day, winter is your season.
