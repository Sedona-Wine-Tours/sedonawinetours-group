There are plenty of things to do in Sedona without hiking, and some of the best views in town come with a parking lot attached. You can drive two nationally designated scenic byways, visit a chapel built into the red rock, stroll a gallery village, stand inside 800-year-old cliff-dwelling country, ride a canyon train and spend an afternoon tasting wine, all without lacing up a single boot. Here is the list we give guests who would rather sip than scramble.

We have guided in Sedona since 2004, and a surprising number of our guests tell us the same thing on the drive out: "Everyone said we had to hike, and we just don't want to." Good news: Sedona was built for looking at, and you can see most of it from a car seat, a patio chair or a short paved path.

## Scenic drives: the easiest red rock views in Sedona

If you only do one thing without hiking, make it a drive. Sedona sits where two of Arizona's prettiest roads meet.

**Red Rock Scenic Byway (State Route 179).** The roughly 14.5-mile road from Interstate 17 into Sedona is an All-American Road, the top tier of the National Scenic Byways Program. [Visit Arizona](https://www.visitarizona.com/like-a-local/short-trips-to-scenic-drives) lists the highlights in order: the Red Rock Ranger District Visitor Center, the Village of Oak Creek, Bell Rock and Courthouse Butte, the Chapel of the Holy Cross and Tlaquepaque. Coming from Phoenix, you get all of it on your way in. Bell Rock has parking areas where you can get the classic photo a few steps from the car.

**Oak Creek Canyon (State Route 89A).** North of Uptown, 89A drops into a narrow, tree-lined canyon. The [Forest Service](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r03/coconino/recreation/oak-creek-canyon-scenic-drive) puts the scenic drive at about 15 miles, roughly an hour, climbing about 2,200 feet past Midgley Bridge and Slide Rock State Park to a set of hairpin switchbacks. About a mile past the top of the switchbacks, Oak Creek Vista overlooks the whole canyon. Late October is peak fall color in the canyon, so this week is a great time to go. If anyone in your car gets carsick, let them ride up front.

A tip from years of doing this: drive 89A north in the morning, when the canyon is quieter, and 179 in late afternoon, when the light turns the rocks orange. Parking on National Forest land around Sedona requires a Red Rock Pass at many sites, so check before you pull over.

## Chapel of the Holy Cross and Sedona's quiet places

**Chapel of the Holy Cross.** This is the one stop nearly every visitor makes, and for good reason. Rancher and sculptor Marguerite Brunswig Staude commissioned the chapel, which was finished in 1956 and is still an active Catholic place of worship, according to [ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/entertainment/absolutely-arizona/chapel-of-the-holy-cross-a-sedona-landmark-for-70-years). Admission is free and donations are welcome. [Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/sedona-secret-7/spiritual/chapel-of-the-holy-cross/) lists hours of 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. daily, with the last car admitted at 4:30 p.m. The lot is small and fills fast in spring and fall, and there is a short uphill walk from the parking area. Go right at opening or in the last hour.

**Amitabha Stupa and Peace Park.** Tucked into the hills of West Sedona at 2650 Pueblo Drive, this 36-foot Buddhist stupa was consecrated in 2004, per the [Tara Mahayana Buddhist Center](https://www.tara.org/amitabha-stupa/). It is free, open dawn to dusk, and the parking lot is open 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. It is a short, gentle walk on marked paths, and you do not need to be Buddhist to sit for a while and take in the view. Vehicles over 20 feet are not allowed.

**Sunset from Airport Mesa.** Drive up Airport Road to the scenic lookout for one of the widest sunset views in town. The lot is small and popular, so arrive 45 minutes before sunset and check the posted parking fee.

## Galleries, shopping and Sedona history

**Tlaquepaque Arts & Shopping Village.** Modeled on a Mexican village, with courtyards, fountains and sycamores along Oak Creek, Tlaquepaque is where we send guests who love art. It is open 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Thursday and 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Friday through Sunday, with restaurants open later, per its [official hours page](https://tlaq.com/hours). Parking is free but limited, and the village is closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas. It is busiest mid-afternoon on weekends.

**Uptown Sedona.** The walkable strip along 89A is touristy, but it is also flat, full of shops and restaurants with patios facing Snoopy Rock and Steamboat Rock, and an easy place to spend an hour. Use the city's municipal lots rather than circling for street spaces.

**Sedona Heritage Museum.** Set in Jordan Historical Park, half a mile up Jordan Road from Uptown, this small museum covers Sedona's homesteaders, cowboys and nearly a century of Hollywood westerns filmed here. [Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/sedona-secret-7/art-culture/sedona-heritage-museum/) lists hours of 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. daily, with an admission fee. It is the best rainy-day stop in town and pairs well with an Uptown lunch.

## Ancient sites and a canyon train ride

**Palatki Heritage Site.** If you want to see cliff dwellings and rock art without a real hike, Palatki is the place. The [Visit Sedona listing](https://visitsedona.com/sedona-secret-7/ancient-trails/palatki-heritage-site/) describes a guided 75-minute tour, limited to 14 people, of a cliff dwelling occupied from about 1150 to 1350 CE plus a large rock-art collection. Know before you go:

- Reservations are required through Recreation.gov (1-877-444-6777).
- From September through May, it is open 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. daily except Wednesdays, when it closes at noon.
- You need a Red Rock Pass or an America the Beautiful pass.
- It is about 30 minutes from Sedona on gravel roads that passenger cars can usually handle in dry weather. Call the Red Rock Ranger District at (928) 203-7500 after rain.
- Expect about 60 uneven stone steps, so it is easy but not flat.

**Montezuma Castle National Monument.** About 30 minutes south near Camp Verde, a short, flat, paved loop leads to one of the best-preserved cliff dwellings in North America. It fits neatly into a wine day on the Verde River side of the valley.

**Verde Canyon Railroad.** From the depot in Clarkdale, the train runs a 40-mile round trip along the Verde River to Perkinsville, through a 680-foot tunnel and past red cliffs you cannot reach by road. Standard 2026 fares are listed at $139 on the [railroad's ticket page](https://verdecanyonrr.com/train-rates/), and the holiday Magical Christmas Journey starts November 27. Book ahead on weekends.

## Wine tasting and stargazing: Sedona after the hike you skipped

Here is where we admit our bias: the Verde Valley is the best non-hiking afternoon in Sedona. Page Springs Road, 15 to 25 minutes from town, has tasting rooms with creekside and vineyard patios, and Old Town Cottonwood puts a cluster of tasting rooms within a few walkable blocks. The red rock views come with you the whole way. Our [wine tasting in Sedona guide](/blog/wine-tasting-in-sedona) breaks down what tasting flights cost, and the [Sedona wineries and vineyards page](/sedona-wineries-and-vineyards) lists where we go.

The one rule: someone has to drive, and it should not be anyone who has been tasting. That is the whole reason we started doing this in 2004. [Wine Tours of Sedona](/wine-tours-of-sedona) runs private, all-inclusive days, and well-behaved dogs are welcome, so the pup does not have to sit at the hotel.

Then, once it is dark, look up. Sedona is an International Dark Sky Community, and on a clear night you can see the Milky Way from a hotel parking lot. Our [Sedona stargazing guide](/blog/sedona-stargazing) covers the best spots and dates.

## A no-hiking day in Sedona, start to finish

If you want it laid out for you, here is the day we would plan for a friend:

1. **8:45 a.m.** Chapel of the Holy Cross, before the lot fills.
2. **9:45 a.m.** Bell Rock photos, then coffee in the Village of Oak Creek.
3. **11 a.m.** Tlaquepaque galleries and lunch.
4. **1 to 5 p.m.** Wine tasting in Page Springs or Old Town Cottonwood, with a driver.
5. **Sunset** Airport Mesa lookout or a patio dinner facing the rocks.
6. **After dark** Step outside and find the Milky Way.

Swap the morning for Palatki or the Verde Canyon Railroad on a second day.

## What to book, what to bring and when to go

**When to go.** October and November, and March through May, bring mild afternoons that make patios and scenic drives perfect. Winter is quieter, with cool days and an occasional dusting of snow on the rocks, which is gorgeous from a car window. Summer afternoons are hot, so put the outdoor stops in the morning.

**What to book.**

- Palatki through Recreation.gov, often days ahead.
- Verde Canyon Railroad seats, especially for weekends and the holiday trains.
- Dinner reservations anywhere with a view.
- A wine tour or a designated driver for any tasting day.

**What to bring.**

- A Red Rock Pass if you plan to park at Forest Service sites.
- Comfortable walking shoes. No hiking, but there are ramps, steps and gallery courtyards.
- Layers. Sedona drops 20 degrees or more after sunset in fall.
- Water, sunscreen and a hat, even in winter. The high desert sun is strong.
