Yes, there are plenty of dog friendly wineries near Sedona, and almost all of them are 20 to 30 minutes away in Verde Valley wine country rather than in town. The rule of thumb is simple: leashed, well-behaved dogs are usually welcome on outdoor patios and grounds, and usually not inside tasting rooms. Here is where we take guests who travel with their dogs, what each place actually allows, and how to plan a wine day the whole family enjoys.

We have been guiding in Sedona since 2004, and some of our favorite tour days are the ones with a dog riding along. A winery patio in the shade of the cottonwoods, a water bowl, a creek breeze and a good flight is about as relaxed as Arizona gets.

## How dog friendly wineries near Sedona set their rules

Before the list, a few things that hold true almost everywhere in the Verde Valley:

- **Outside yes, inside usually no.** Most wineries that welcome dogs mean the patio, lawn or grounds. Health rules and food service keep pets out of most tasting rooms and bistros. Service dogs are a different matter and are generally welcome inside.
- **Leashes, always.** Expect to keep your dog on a leash the entire visit. These are working farms with vines, equipment, irrigation and other guests' dogs.
- **Policies change.** A patio that welcomed dogs last spring may close it for a private event this weekend. Call ahead on the day, especially on Saturdays and during festivals.
- **One dog per person is a good guide.** Two adults with two calm dogs is easy. A party of six with four dogs needs a winery that has room for it, so ask first.

## Page Springs and Cornville: the creekside wineries

Page Springs Road in Cornville is the closest wine country to Sedona, roughly 20 to 30 minutes from town, and it is the most scenic place to bring a dog: shady grounds, Oak Creek running past, and wineries within about a mile of each other. Our [Page Springs wine tasting guide](/blog/page-springs-wine-tasting) covers the whole corridor; here is the dog-specific version.

**Page Springs Cellars.** Leashed dogs are welcome on the outside grounds, but non-service dogs are not allowed inside the tasting room, according to the winery's own [FAQ page](https://pagespringscellars.com/faqs/). Properly trained service dogs are welcome inside. The tasting room is open 365 days a year, and tastings are reserved through Tock; parties larger than six should give advance notice. The posted hours have shifted this year, so check the current hours on the winery's site before you go.

**Oak Creek Vineyards & Winery.** Just up Page Springs Road at 1555 Page Springs Rd., Oak Creek Vineyards openly invites guests to "bring the entire crew, kids and fur babies included" on its [website](https://www.oakcreekvineyardswine.com/), and [Visit Page Springs](https://visitpagesprings.com/) lists it with a pet-friendly patio. It is one of the easiest stops on a dog day: casual, outdoors and close to the other Page Springs wineries. Call (928) 660-6935 for current hours and tasting prices.

**Alcantara Vineyards.** Out on the Verde River near Cottonwood, Alcantara has hosted dog-centered events; the [Verde Independent](https://www.verdenews.com/news/tail-wagging-good-time-at-alcantara-vineyards/article_0bb21ce5-6bb8-41b7-b8c2-e921d5a3f5f6.html) covered an April 18, 2026 afternoon where big and small dogs, some in costume, roamed with guests. Everyday policies can differ from event days, so call ahead before bringing your dog on a regular visit.

**The rest of the corridor.** Javelina Leap, DA Ranch and Cove Mesa Vineyard round out the Page Springs and Cornville stops. Each sets its own rules, and we would rather you hear them from the winery than from a blog, so a quick call is the way to go. If you are with us, we handle that call for you.

## Old Town Cottonwood: walkable tasting rooms with patios

[Old Town Cottonwood](/blog/old-town-cottonwood-wine-tasting) is a different kind of wine day: about four flat blocks of North Main Street, roughly 30 minutes from Sedona, with tasting rooms, wine bars and restaurants side by side. For dog owners, the appeal is that you park once and walk the dog between stops.

**DA Vines Vineyard.** The DA Vines tasting room at 705 N Main St., Suite A, is listed as pet friendly by [Visit Arizona](https://visitarizona.com/directory/da-vines-vineyard), and [Visit Page Springs](https://visitpagesprings.com/) notes its dog-friendly patio. The Old Town room has been closed Mondays and Tuesdays, with a Sunday brunch schedule, so check current hours at (928) 634-6710.

**Other Main Street rooms.** Many Old Town tasting rooms have a sidewalk table or small patio, and some will bring a flight out to you while your dog waits in the shade. Others are indoor-only. Walk the block, look for a water bowl by the door, and ask; staff on Main Street are used to the question.

**A dog break between tastings.** [Dead Horse Ranch State Park](https://azstateparks.com/dead-horse/explore/facility-information) sits on the Verde River just north of Old Town, with lagoons and riverside trails. Pets must be leashed and may not be left unattended, and they are not permitted in park buildings. Fall and winter day-use hours are 7 a.m. to 6 p.m., and day entry is $20 for a vehicle with two to four people. A 30-minute walk there before your first tasting settles most dogs down nicely.

## A sample dog-friendly wine day

Here is the shape of a day we would plan for two people and one dog, adjusted to the season:

1. **Late morning:** a short, leashed walk at Dead Horse Ranch or along the creek so your dog gets the wiggles out.
2. **First tasting, around noon:** a Page Springs patio with shade, before the afternoon crowd arrives.
3. **Second stop:** a second creekside winery a mile up the road, with a water break in between.
4. **Late afternoon:** Old Town Cottonwood, where you can stroll Main Street, find a patio table and grab an early dinner.
5. **Back in Sedona by sunset,** with nobody having driven after tasting.

Three stops is the sweet spot with a dog. Four starts to feel long for most pups, and for most people too.

## What to book, what to bring and when to go

**When to go.** Late October through April is the best season for wine tasting with a dog. Days are mild, patios are comfortable at midday and the creek corridor is beautiful in fall color. From late spring through early fall, plan patio stops for the morning and skip hot pavement and gravel in the afternoon, when surfaces get too hot for paws. Weekdays are calmer than Saturdays at nearly every winery.

**What to book.**
- Your tastings, if the winery takes reservations, and a note that a dog is coming.
- Pet-friendly lodging confirmed in writing, including any fee and whether dogs can be left alone in the room. Our [dog-friendly Sedona guide](/blog/dog-friendly-sedona) covers lodging, trails and parks.
- A driver. With Wine Tours of Sedona or Sedona Wine Adventures, use code DOGFRIENDLY.

**What to bring.**
- A standard 6-foot leash. Retractable leashes and crowded patios do not mix.
- A collapsible water bowl and more water than you think you need. The air is dry here even in winter.
- A mat or small towel so your dog has a place to settle under the table.
- Waste bags, and a chew or treat to keep things calm while you taste.
- A vaccination record on your phone, in case a winery or kennel asks.

**Patio manners.** Keep your dog close to your table, away from food service and other guests' dogs unless both owners agree. If your dog is having a rough day, the kind thing is to cut a stop; the wine will still be there.
