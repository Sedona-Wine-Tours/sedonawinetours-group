A Jerome Arizona day trip is one of the easiest and most rewarding half-days you can add to a Sedona visit: the old copper town sits about 30 miles southwest of Sedona, roughly 45 minutes to an hour by car, clinging to the side of Cleopatra Hill at more than 5,000 feet. In a few hours you can tour a 1916 mining mansion, stand on glass over a 1,900-foot mine shaft, wander a Main Street full of galleries and tasting rooms, and be back in Sedona for sunset. October is one of the best months to go, with cool afternoons, long views across the Verde Valley and a town that leans happily into its haunted reputation around Halloween.

We have been driving guests through the Verde Valley since 2004, and Jerome is the stop people talk about on the ride home. Here is how we would plan it.

## Sedona to Jerome: the drive and how long it takes

The route is simple. Take State Route 89A southwest out of West Sedona, through the open high desert toward Cottonwood, then follow 89A through Clarkdale and up the hill into Jerome. Figure about 30 miles and 45 minutes to an hour, depending on traffic through Cottonwood and how often you pull over for the view of the red rocks behind you.

The last few miles climb a series of switchbacks with tight turns and steep grades, and Jerome itself is built so steeply that its streets stack on top of each other. Take it slow, watch for pedestrians, and if you are driving a large RV, consider leaving it in Cottonwood.

If you keep going on 89A past Jerome, the road climbs over Mingus Mountain toward Prescott. It is a beautiful drive in the fall, but it is narrow and winding, and it adds real time. For a Sedona-based day trip, most people come back the way they came.

## Why Jerome is worth the trip

Jerome was incorporated in 1899 and, according to the [Town of Jerome](https://jerome.az.gov/jerome-then-and-now), grew to nearly 15,000 people during its copper boom in the early 1900s. Major mining stopped in 1953, the population collapsed, and Jerome earned its "ghost town" nickname. Today it is home to only a few hundred residents, plus a lot of artists, restaurateurs and winemakers who have turned those brick and stone buildings into one of the most interesting small towns in Arizona.

What makes it special is the setting. From almost anywhere on Main Street you look straight down across the Verde Valley to Cottonwood, Clarkdale, the Verde River and, on a clear day, the red rocks of Sedona. Some buildings have slid downhill over the decades (the old jail is the famous example), and the whole town feels a little tilted, in the best way.

## Things to do in Jerome AZ

### Jerome State Historic Park and the Douglas Mansion

Start at the top. [Jerome State Historic Park](https://azstateparks.com/jerome) is built around the Douglas Mansion, which James S. Douglas built in 1916 on the hill above his Little Daisy Mine. It served as both a family home and a hotel for mining officials and investors, and it was remarkably modern for its time. [Visit Arizona](https://www.visitarizona.com/like-a-local/48-hours-in-jerome-and-cottonwood) points out the wine cellar, billiard room, marble shower, steam heat and central vacuum system.

Inside today is a museum on Jerome's mining history, with photographs, mining tools, minerals and a short film that explains how the town came to be. The front porch and grounds have one of the best views in the valley. The park is typically open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. daily with paid admission; check the current hours and fees on the [Arizona State Parks page](https://azstateparks.com/jerome) before you go.

### Audrey Headframe Park

This is the one everybody photographs. Right next to the road below the state park, the Audrey Headframe is the largest wooden headframe still standing in Arizona, finished in 1918. Next to it is a glass platform where you can stand over the Audrey shaft, which [Arizona State Parks](https://azstateparks.com/jerome/things-to-do) says is 1,900 feet deep, deeper than the Empire State Building is tall by a wide margin. The park is open daily from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. It takes about 15 minutes, and it is well worth it.

### Jerome Historical Society Mine Museum

On Main Street across from the Connor Hotel, the [Mine Museum](https://www.jeromehistoricalsociety.com/museums-buildings/mine-museum/) is a small, packed museum inside a former saloon. It covers Jerome's timeline from mining camp to arts town, with drills, carbide lamps, ore carts, saloon gambling pieces and displays on the Chinese, Italian, Mexican, Irish, Slavic and other immigrant communities who built the town. The Historical Society lists it as open daily from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., with admission of $2 for adults and free for kids under 12. That might be the best $2 you spend in the Verde Valley.

### Galleries and shops on Main Street

Jerome has a big arts scene for a town this size. Visit Arizona highlights the Jerome Artists Cooperative Gallery at 502 Main St., featuring work by more than 30 local artists, and Firefly Gallery at 208 Main St. You will also find copper jewelry, pottery, kaleidoscopes, vintage shops and plenty of places to duck into when the wind picks up. Plan on more time than you think; the shops are tucked onto several levels and side streets.

## Jerome wine tasting and where to eat

Wine is a big part of Jerome now, and it is a natural fit for a day built around the Verde Valley. The best-known stop is the [Caduceus Cellars](https://caduceus.org/) tasting room at 158 Main St., which pours flights of its Caduceus and Merkin Vineyards wines and has a bottle shop. Caduceus lists hours of 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday through Thursday and 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. on Friday and Saturday. You will find other tasting rooms along Main Street as well, and hours change by season, so check ahead if a particular room is on your list.

For food, Visit Arizona's Jerome guide names a few local favorites:

- **The Mine Café** for a homestyle breakfast before you start walking.
- **Haunted Hamburger**, with a patio overlooking Jerome, Cottonwood and the Sedona red rocks in the distance.
- **The Clinkscale** for a chef-driven dinner with local wine and craft beer.
- **The Spirit Room** in the Connor Hotel for a drink and live music.

Check the current hours for each, especially early in the week, when some Jerome restaurants close.

A practical note: if your group plans to taste in Jerome and then keep going to the wineries in Cottonwood, Cornville and Page Springs, that is exactly when a designated driver earns their keep. The switchbacks into and out of Jerome are not the place to be second-guessing your last pour.

## Jerome in October and Halloween

Jerome's mining-camp history and creaky hillside buildings have made it one of Arizona's favorite places to celebrate Halloween, and October weekends get busy. Arizona State Parks has hosted evening "Flashfright" tours of the Douglas Mansion in late October in recent years, letting visitors explore the mansion after dark, including spaces not normally open. The dates and prices change each year (the 2025 event was held October 18), and as of this writing the 2026 date had not been posted, so check the [Arizona State Parks Halloween events page](https://azstateparks.com/halloween) and the park's events listings before you plan around it.

If you are in town the week of Halloween, expect costumes, crowds and full parking lots, especially on the Saturday closest to October 31. It is a lot of fun, and it rewards an early arrival.

## When to go, what to bring and parking

**When to go.** Weekday mornings are the calmest. Fall and spring are ideal: Jerome runs noticeably cooler than Phoenix and a little cooler than Sedona's lower neighborhoods, and the views are sharpest after a front passes through. Summer afternoons are warm but tolerable at 5,000 feet. Winter can bring wind and the occasional dusting of snow.

**Parking.** The [Town of Jerome](https://jerome.az.gov/parking-and-shuttle-service) runs four pay-to-park zones, including the lower lot by the Sliding Jail, the Hull Avenue lot and the east side of Main Street. Parking there is $5 for the whole day, charged from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., paid by credit card at the kiosks. Vehicles with disability placards park free. Free parking is available in the large "300 Level" lot on Perkinsville Road and in some spots along Main Street, and the town runs a free shuttle on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and holidays.

**What to bring.**

- Comfortable shoes with good grip. Jerome is all hills and stairs.
- A layer or windbreaker; the wind can come up the hill fast in the afternoon.
- Water, sunscreen and a hat.
- A card for parking kiosks, and a little cash for small shops.
- A leash if your dog is coming. Main Street is a fun walk with a pup, but check pet rules at each museum before you go in.

**A sample half-day.** Leave Sedona around 9 a.m., park and walk up to the Douglas Mansion and Audrey Headframe first, have lunch on Main Street, spend the early afternoon in the galleries and the Mine Museum, then taste a flight before heading back down the hill. On the way home, the [tasting rooms of Old Town Cottonwood](/blog/old-town-cottonwood-wine-tasting) and Tuzigoot National Monument are both within a few minutes of 89A.

## Make it a Verde Valley day

Jerome pairs beautifully with the rest of the Verde Valley.
