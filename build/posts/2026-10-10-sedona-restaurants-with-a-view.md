The best Sedona restaurants with a view fall into three camps: high perches that look across the whole red rock skyline (Mariposa and Mesa Grill), canyon settings where the cliffs are right overhead (Enchantment Resort in Boynton Canyon), and creekside tables under the sycamores (Cress on Oak Creek, and Page Springs Cellars out in wine country). In October the sun sets around 6 PM, so a 5 PM reservation on a west-facing patio is the move. We have been steering guests to these tables since 2004, and here is how we would plan the meal.

## When to book a sunset dinner in Sedona this fall

Arizona does not observe daylight saving time, so Sedona's sunset slides steadily earlier through the fall with no clock change to throw you off. By our calculation for Sedona's coordinates, sunset lands at roughly:

- **October 10:** about 6:00 PM
- **October 20:** about 5:47 PM
- **October 31:** about 5:35 PM
- **November 15:** about 5:22 PM

The red rocks put on their best color in the 30 to 45 minutes before the sun drops, not after it. So for a sunset dinner, book your table about an hour before sunset and plan to linger. In late October that means a 4:30 or 4:45 PM seating, which sounds early until you watch Cathedral Rock turn copper with a glass in your hand.

One more fall detail: evenings cool off fast at 4,500 feet. A patio that feels perfect at 5 PM can feel like a jacket-and-heater situation by 7. Bring a layer, and ask for a heater table when you book.

## High perches: the big-sky restaurants with a view

**Mariposa Latin Inspired Grill** is the restaurant most locals name first when someone asks for the view. It sits on a bluff at 700 State Route 89A, right at the gateway between West Sedona and Uptown, and looks out across a long sweep of red rock. Chef Lisa Dahl cooks South American-inspired food over a wood-fired grill and wood-burning oven. Visit Sedona lists 130 outdoor seats, lunch from 11:30 AM to 2:30 PM and dinner from 5 PM daily, with reservations recommended ([Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/directory/mariposa-latin-inspired-grill/)). For sunset, ask for the patio and book well ahead on fall weekends.

**Mesa Grill** sits at the Sedona Airport, 1185 Airport Mesa Road, on top of Airport Mesa above West Sedona. Floor-to-ceiling windows look out over the runway toward Cathedral Rock, Courthouse Butte and Schnebly Hill, and the window tables fill first ([Sedona Monthly](https://sedonamonthly.com/2014/mesa-grill-sedona-airport/)). It is a more relaxed, burgers-to-steaks kind of place, which makes it a good pick for families or a casual breakfast with a view. Check the current hours before you go, since airport-mesa traffic and parking get busy near sunset.

A tip if you eat at either: the Airport Mesa overlook nearby is one of Sedona's classic sunset spots, and it is crowded. Watching from a dinner table is the more comfortable way to see the same light.

## Canyon views: Enchantment Resort in Boynton Canyon

If you want the cliffs close enough to feel them, drive out to **Enchantment Resort** at the end of Boynton Canyon Road. The resort's dining is open to the public seasonally, and public diners need a reservation, by phone at 844-361-9123 or through OpenTable ([Enchantment Resort](https://enchantmentresort.com/dining)).

- **Che Ah Chi** is the signature room: modern American cooking, canyon views and an extensive wine list. It serves breakfast, lunch and dinner, and it sits, as Sedona Monthly put it, "in the shadow of Boynton Canyon's red rocks."
- **Tii Gavo** is the casual option, Southwestern food served indoors or on the patio by the firepit. On a cool October evening, that firepit is a big part of the appeal.

Enchantment is a resort with a gate, so leave extra time for arrival and parking, and confirm your reservation the day before. It is also worth noting that the resort has been updating its rooms and restaurant concepts ([Sedona Monthly](https://sedonamonthly.com/2026/northern-arizona-dining-whats-new-in-town/)), so check the dining page for what is open on your dates.

## Creekside tables: dining on Oak Creek

Not every great Sedona view is a red rock skyline. Some of our favorite meals in town happen at creek level, where the sound of the water does half the work.

**Cress on Oak Creek** at L'Auberge de Sedona sets tables on the banks of Oak Creek, under the sycamores, a short walk below Uptown. According to the resort, dinner runs daily from 5 to 9 PM (last seating), breakfast and lunch are served Monday through Saturday, Sunday brunch runs 7 AM to 2 PM, and the Cress Bar is open daily from 2 to 9 PM. Reservations are strongly recommended ([L'Auberge de Sedona](https://lauberge.com/cress-on-oak-creek)). For a special-occasion dinner, this is where we send people. In late October, as the sycamores start to turn gold, it is hard to beat.

If a long dinner is not in the plans, the Cress Bar's afternoon hours make it an easy stop for a glass of wine by the creek before you head back out.

## Uptown patios and a newcomer

**Cowboy Club** at 241 N State Route 89A is an Uptown institution with about 40 seasonal outdoor seats and a menu that leans Southwestern. Visit Sedona lists it as pet-friendly, which makes it one of the easier patio meals in Uptown if you are traveling with your dog ([Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/directory/cowboy-club)). Reservations are recommended, especially for the patio on fall weekends. Call ahead to confirm the current dog policy for outdoor seating.

**Lucida** is the newest name on our list. It opened with the Outbound Sedona Hotel (the former Poco Diablo) in May 2026 and serves Southwestern dishes, with indoor seating and an outdoor patio opening onto the courtyard ([Sedona Monthly](https://sedonamonthly.com/2026/northern-arizona-dining-whats-new-in-town/)). It is more of a garden setting than a panorama, but it is worth knowing about if you are staying on the south end of town near the Village of Oak Creek.

## Wine with a view: Page Springs Cellars

Since this is our home turf, we would be remiss not to mention the view from Verde Valley wine country. **Page Springs Cellars**, at 1500 N. Page Springs Road in Cornville, about half an hour from Sedona, sits right on Oak Creek. The Tasting Room & Bistro serves a French-inspired bistro menu, and the winery is open 365 days a year, with weekdays generally quieter than weekends ([Page Springs Cellars](https://pagespringscellars.com/visit/)). Creek access is limited to winery guests with a conservation wristband during the summer season, so check the current rules if you want to wander down to the water.

A lunch and tasting on the creek, followed by sunset dinner back in Sedona, is about as good a fall day as Arizona offers. It is also exactly the kind of day we build when guests want wine without anyone in the group having to drive. You can see the full lineup on our [Sedona wineries and vineyards guide](/sedona-wineries-and-vineyards).

## What to book, what to bring and when to go

**What to book**

- Reserve sunset tables at least a week ahead on fall weekends, and further out for Saturday nights in October and November. Ask specifically for the patio or a window.
- Enchantment requires reservations for public diners. Mariposa, Cress and Cowboy Club recommend them.
- If wine is part of the day, book transportation too. Sedona's restaurants with a view are spread across Airport Mesa, Uptown, Boynton Canyon and Cornville, and the drives between them are dark, winding two-lane roads after sunset. Our [Wine Tours of Sedona](/wine-tours-of-sedona) itineraries and private charters handle the driving, and our [groups, corporate and wedding](/groups-corporate-weddings) team can plan rehearsal dinners and celebrations around a sunset reservation.

**What to bring**

- A warm layer. Fall evenings cool off quickly once the sun is down.
- Sunglasses for the first half of a west-facing sunset dinner. That low sun is bright.
- For dogs: water, a leash and a mat, and a quick call ahead to confirm the patio policy.

**When to go**

- **October:** Peak patio weather. Book early, since it is also peak season.
- **Early to mid-November:** Cooler evenings, gold sycamores along Oak Creek, and slightly easier reservations on weeknights. Our [Sedona in November guide](/blog/sedona-in-november) covers the rest of the month.
- **Any time of year:** The hour before sunset is the reservation to get.
