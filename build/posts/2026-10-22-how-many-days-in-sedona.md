How many days in Sedona is enough? For most first-time visitors, three days is the sweet spot: one day for the classic red rock hikes and viewpoints, one for Oak Creek Canyon and the galleries, and one for Verde Valley wine country and the ancient sites. Two days covers the highlights if you plan well, and one day is a good sampler, not a full visit.

We have been guiding in Sedona since 2004, and "how long should we stay?" is the question we hear most from people still building their trip. Here is how we would answer it for this fall, with three itineraries you can use as written or mix and match.

## How many days in Sedona do you really need?

It depends on what you want out of the trip, but here is the honest breakdown:

- **One day:** You will see Sedona, not experience it. Good if you are passing through on the way to the Grand Canyon or coming up from Phoenix for a day.
- **Two days:** Enough for one solid hike, Oak Creek Canyon, a sunset and a good dinner or two. This is the classic Sedona weekend trip.
- **Three days:** The right answer for most people. You get the red rocks, the canyon and a full, unhurried day in wine country, and you are not racing the sun every afternoon.
- **Four or more:** Add a [Grand Canyon day trip](/blog/sedona-to-grand-canyon-day-trip), a drive up to Jerome and Prescott, or simply slow down. Sedona rewards slowing down.

One fall-specific note before you plan: Arizona does not change its clocks, so daylight shrinks fast. By November, the sun sets around 5:30 p.m. or earlier, which means your hiking window is shorter than you might expect. Front-load the active stuff in the morning and save the afternoons for tasting rooms, galleries and sunset.

## Is one day in Sedona enough? A one-day itinerary

If one day is all you have, pick a lane and do it well. Here is the day we would build:

**Morning: one red rock hike.** Start early, ideally on the trail by 8 a.m., because popular trailhead lots fill fast on fall weekends. Devil's Bridge is the hike most visitors ask about. [Arizona Highways](https://arizonahighways.com/devils-bridge-trail) lists it at 3.8 miles round-trip from the Mescal Trailhead off Long Canyon Road, uphill the whole way to the arch, with about 450 feet of climbing. Skip the shortcut on Forest Road 152 unless you are in a high-clearance vehicle built for it; Arizona Highways describes that road as sharp rocks, big boulders and deep ruts. If you want something gentler, the paths around Bell Rock give you big views with less effort.

Most Forest Service trailheads around town require a Red Rock Pass. According to [Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/trip-planning/red-rock-pass-program-qa/), it costs $5 a day, $15 a week or $20 a year, and an America the Beautiful pass works in its place. We cover where you need one in our [Red Rock Pass guide](/blog/red-rock-pass-sedona).

**Midday: lunch and a stroll.** Head to Tlaquepaque Arts & Shopping Village at 336 State Route 179, a Mexican-village-style courtyard of galleries, shops and restaurants. Its [visitor page](https://www.tlaq.com/visitor-information) lists general hours of 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., though individual shops and restaurants keep their own.

**Afternoon: wine country.** The closest wineries are about 20 to 30 minutes from town on Page Springs Road. Two or three tasting rooms is the right number for an afternoon. Our [Page Springs wine tasting guide](/blog/page-springs-wine-tasting) covers each one.

**Evening: sunset and dinner.** Be back in Sedona for sunset, then dinner. If you only have one evening, make it a restaurant with a view; we listed our favorites in [Sedona restaurants with a view](/blog/sedona-restaurants-with-a-view).

## 2 days in Sedona: the classic weekend itinerary

With 2 days in Sedona, split the trip between the red rocks and the canyon, and give wine its own afternoon.

**Day one: red rocks and sunset.**

- Morning hike: Devil's Bridge, or the trails around Bell Rock and Courthouse Butte for something flatter.
- Lunch in West Sedona, where most of the locals eat.
- Afternoon: Tlaquepaque and the galleries along 179.
- Sunset from a viewpoint or a patio, then dinner Uptown.

**Day two: Oak Creek Canyon and wine country.**

- Morning: drive north on 89A into Oak Creek Canyon. Peak fall color in the canyon usually runs from the last week of October into early November.
- Stop at Slide Rock State Park about 7 miles north of town. [Arizona State Parks](https://azstateparks.com/slide-rock/) lists hours from November 1 to December 1 of 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., with entry closing at 5 p.m., and an off-season fee of $10 per vehicle for one to four adults. Entry is first come, first served by vehicle only, with no walk-ins or drop-offs, and the park [closes the lot](https://azstateparks.com/press/entry-changes-coming-to-slide-rock-state-park-on-may-1) when its roughly 156 spaces fill. Arrive early.
- Afternoon: head south to Page Springs or Old Town Cottonwood for tastings and an early dinner.

A note on West Fork: the canyon's most famous fall hike is closed. The [Coconino National Forest closure order](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r03/coconino/alerts/west-fork-temporary-area-closure-extended) covers West Fork Trail No. 108 and the drainage through October 31, 2026, because of flood and rockfall hazards after this summer's Pocket Fire. Check the order before you go if your trip is in early November, and see our [West Fork update](/blog/is-west-fork-trail-open) for alternatives.

## A 3 day Sedona itinerary: the one we recommend

Three days lets you add the Verde Valley, which is where many of our guests say the trip really clicked. Use the two-day plan above for days one and two, but move wine country to day three and give it the whole day.

**Day three: Verde Valley wine country and ancient sites.**

- **Morning: a cliff dwelling or rock art site.** Montezuma Castle National Monument is about 35 to 45 minutes south of Uptown and one of the best-preserved cliff dwellings in North America; we covered hours and fees in our [Montezuma Castle guide](/blog/montezuma-castle-national-monument). Closer to Sedona, Palatki Heritage Site runs timed, guided tours of cliff dwellings and rock art. According to [Recreation.gov](https://recreation.gov/ticket/facility/10089352), tickets are required and released 120 days ahead, October-to-May hours are 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. (shorter on Wednesdays), and each vehicle also needs a Red Rock Pass or federal pass. When we checked this week, the ticket page showed a closure notice, so confirm it is open before you plan around it.
- **Midday: lunch in Old Town Cottonwood.** Main Street is walkable and full of tasting rooms; our [Old Town Cottonwood wine tasting guide](/blog/old-town-cottonwood-wine-tasting) maps it out.
- **Afternoon: Page Springs Road.** Finish at the estate wineries along Oak Creek, where you can taste where the grapes are grown.
- **Evening:** a slow dinner and, if the sky is clear, stargazing. Sedona is an International Dark Sky Community, and fall nights are crisp and clear.

This is also the day where a driver makes the biggest difference. Wine country is spread out across two-lane roads that get dark early in fall, and nobody in your group should be counting pours. Our [Wine Tours of Sedona](/wine-tours-of-sedona) private tours and [SIP Sedona](/sip-sedona) tasting tours both handle the driving and the timing.

## What to book, what to bring and when to go

**What to book ahead:**

- Dinner reservations, especially sunset tables on Friday and Saturday nights in October and November.
- Palatki tickets, if the site is open during your trip.
- A wine tour, if you want a driver. Fall weekends book up; you can compare options with our [Tour Chooser](/#chooser) or call Sedona Wine Tours at (928) 504-2445.
- Lodging, which is tightest on fall weekends and Thanksgiving week. Our [where to stay in Sedona guide](/blog/where-to-stay-in-sedona) breaks down Uptown, West Sedona, the Village of Oak Creek and Cottonwood.

**What to bring:**

- Layers. Fall mornings are cold and afternoons are warm.
- Real hiking shoes and more water than you think you need, even in cool weather.
- A Red Rock Pass or America the Beautiful pass for trailhead parking.
- Photo ID for everyone planning to taste wine.
- A headlamp if there is any chance you will be on a trail near sunset.

**When to go:** October and November are prime season, with mild days and fall color in Oak Creek Canyon. Weekdays are noticeably calmer than weekends, so if you can, shift your three days to a Sunday-through-Tuesday or Tuesday-through-Thursday stay.

## A few local tips to stretch your time

- **Start early.** Trailhead parking is the single biggest time thief in Sedona. On the trail by 8 a.m. beats circling a full lot at 10.
- **Don't over-schedule the afternoons.** The light is best late in the day. Leave room to sit somewhere and watch the rocks change color.
- **Group nearby stops.** West Sedona trails pair with Tlaquepaque; Oak Creek Canyon pairs with Uptown; Page Springs pairs with Cornville and Cottonwood.
- **Traveling with a dog?** Many Sedona trails and patios welcome leashed dogs; our [dog-friendly Sedona guide](/blog/dog-friendly-sedona) has the details. Note that Palatki does not allow pets beyond the parking lot.
