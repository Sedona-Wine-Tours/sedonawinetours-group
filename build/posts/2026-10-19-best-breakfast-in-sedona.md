The best breakfast in Sedona depends on what the rest of your day looks like: Coffee Pot Restaurant in West Sedona opens at 6 AM for hikers who want an early start, Creekside American Bistro and Mesa Grill trade a little speed for red rock views, and Indian Gardens in Oak Creek Canyon is the easy grab-and-go before a canyon drive. If you have a wine day ahead, pick a real sit-down breakfast with some protein in it, because tasting rooms pour on an empty stomach faster than you would think.

We have been picking guests up for wine tours since 2004, and "where should we eat breakfast?" is one of the questions we hear most, usually the night before. Here is the short list we actually give people, sorted by what kind of morning you are planning. Hours below are what each restaurant publishes as of mid-October 2026; breakfast spots here change hours with the seasons, so check the current hours on the linked site before you go.

## Best breakfast in Sedona for an early start: Coffee Pot Restaurant

If you want to be at a trailhead by 8, **Coffee Pot Restaurant** is the answer. It is open daily from 6 AM to 2 PM and serves breakfast the whole time, with lunch added from 11 AM. It calls itself the "Home of 101 Omelettes," and the menu really is that long ([Coffee Pot Restaurant](https://coffeepotsedona.com/faq/)).

A few things worth knowing before you go:

- **No reservations.** Parties of any size are seated in the order they sign in. The line builds fast on fall weekends, so the 6 to 7:30 AM window is your friend.
- **Parking is tight.** The restaurant's own lot is small. Its FAQ points guests to the Sedona Center lot next door (home of the Mary D. Fisher Theatre) and, if that is full, the Bashas' lot.
- **It is on 89A in West Sedona**, at 2050 W State Route 89A across from the movie theater, which puts you minutes from the Dry Creek Road and Boynton Canyon trailheads.
- **Dogs stay home.** Pets are not allowed inside or on the patio, since guests walk through the dining room to reach it.

The restaurant also notes that it has many gluten-free items but no dedicated gluten-free kitchen, and that nuts show up in several popular dishes, which is useful to know if anyone in your group has dietary needs.

## Brunch in Sedona with a view: Creekside and Mesa Grill

When people ask for breakfast with a view, we send them to one of these two. Both come from the same local restaurant group and both take reservations, which matters a lot on a busy October weekend.

**Creekside American Bistro** sits on State Route 179 at the edge of Uptown, among the sycamores along Oak Creek with the red rocks overhead. Breakfast runs daily from 7:30 to 11 AM, then brunch and lunch from 11 AM to 4 PM. The restaurant leans into pancakes and omelets for breakfast, and you can reserve through OpenTable or by calling (928) 282-1705 ([Creekside American Bistro](https://creeksidesedona.com/breakfast/)). Sedona Monthly also calls out its brunch cocktails, if your morning is more leisurely than ours usually are ([Sedona Monthly](https://sedonamonthly.com/2026/best-breakfast-brunch-sedona/)).

**Mesa Grill Sedona** is up on Airport Mesa at 1185 Airport Road, next to the Airport Scenic Lookout. Breakfast, brunch and lunch run 7:30 AM to 3 PM daily, and the room and patio look out across the whole valley while small planes land past the tables. The patio is pet-friendly, which makes it one of the better dog-friendly breakfast options in town. Reservations are available by phone at (928) 282-2400 or on OpenTable ([Mesa Grill Sedona](https://mesagrillsedona.com/)).

A practical tip: Airport Mesa parking fills early with sunrise and vortex visitors. If you are eating at Mesa Grill, plan to park at the restaurant and walk to the overlook afterward rather than circling the small lookout lot. If you want the same views at dinner, our post on [Sedona restaurants with a view](/blog/sedona-restaurants-with-a-view) covers the sunset version of this list.

## Breakfast in Oak Creek Canyon: Indian Gardens Cafe & Market

Heading north on 89A toward Slide Rock or Flagstaff? **Indian Gardens Cafe & Market** is right on the highway in the canyon at 3951 N State Route 89A, about four miles up from Uptown. It is open every day from 8 AM to 4 PM, with the kitchen closing at 3 PM, and it serves breakfast and lunch with carry-out available ([Indian Gardens](https://www.indiangardens.com/)).

We like it for a coffee and a breakfast sandwich before a canyon drive, and it is a natural first stop if you are chasing fall color. The leaves in Oak Creek Canyon usually peak from late October into early November. One reminder: West Fork Trail is closed through October 31, 2026 because of hazards left by this summer's Pocket Fire, so read our [West Fork closure update](/blog/is-west-fork-trail-open) before you plan a canyon morning around that hike.

## A slower morning in Uptown and Tlaquepaque

If your morning is about wandering instead of hiking, **The Secret Garden Cafe** at Tlaquepaque Arts & Shopping Village is a pleasant place to start. According to its Visit Sedona listing, breakfast runs 8 to 11 AM, followed by lunch and dinner, and the menu includes healthy and vegan dishes along with specialty coffee. Most of its seating is outdoors, around 87 patio seats compared with 22 inside, and it is listed as pet-friendly. Call (928) 203-9564 to confirm where dogs can sit ([Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/directory/secret-garden-cafe-at-tlaquepaque/)).

Eat, then walk the courtyards when the galleries open. In late October that also puts you right in the middle of Tlaquepaque's Day of the Dead preparations, which we covered in our [Halloween in Sedona guide](/blog/halloween-in-sedona).

## Village of Oak Creek and Cottonwood options

Not everyone stays in Sedona proper, and the two most common bases outside town each have a solid local breakfast.

**Miley's Cafe** in the Village of Oak Creek, at 7000 State Route 179, is a good pick if you are staying south of town or driving up from Phoenix. Visit Sedona describes a menu of American breakfast standards with homemade hash browns and gravy, banana French toast and cinnamon rolls, alongside huevos rancheros and chilaquiles. Its listing does not post hours, so call (928) 284-4123 before you go ([Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/directory/mileys-cafe/)). From the Village, you are a short drive from the Bell Rock and Courthouse Butte trailheads.

**Red Rooster Cafe** at 901 N Main Street sits in Old Town Cottonwood, and its "Big O'l Breakfast Burrito" is the thing people talk about ([Verde River Institute](https://verderiver.org/business-directory/red-rooster-cafe)). Check the current hours directly with the cafe. If you are staying in Cottonwood for a wine weekend, it pairs well with an afternoon on Main Street; our [Old Town Cottonwood wine tasting guide](/blog/old-town-cottonwood-wine-tasting) walks through the tasting rooms there.

## What to eat before a wine tour (from people who drive one every day)

This is the part we care most about, because it is where we see good days go sideways. Our advice to every group:

- **Eat a real breakfast.** Eggs, potatoes, a burrito, an omelet: something with protein and some fat. A pastry and a latte will not carry you through four or five tastings.
- **Give yourself time.** Breakfast spots on a fall weekend can mean a 30 to 45 minute wait. If your pickup is mid-morning, a reservation at Creekside or Mesa Grill, or an early arrival at Coffee Pot, keeps you from rushing.
- **Drink water at breakfast, not just coffee.** Sedona sits at about 4,350 feet and the air is dry. Our guests who start hydrated finish the day feeling much better.
- **Skip the mimosa if you have a full tasting day.** Save it for brunch on your day off.
- **Grab a snack for the road.** Many Verde Valley tasting rooms serve food, but not all of them, and lunch timing varies by itinerary.

On our tours we keep bottled water in the vehicle and build itineraries around food stops, but nothing replaces a good breakfast before you get in. If you are traveling with a group or celebrating something, our [groups, corporate and weddings page](/groups-corporate-weddings) explains how we time a day for bigger parties.

## When to go, what to book, what to bring

**When to go:** Before 8 AM on weekends from October through mid-November, Sedona's busiest stretch of fall. Weekday mornings are noticeably calmer. Most of the places above serve breakfast only until 11 AM, so a late start can mean you are ordering from the lunch menu.

**What to book:** Reserve Creekside or Mesa Grill a day or two ahead on fall weekends. Coffee Pot does not take reservations, so arrive early. For everywhere else, a quick call confirms hours, which shift with the season.

**What to bring:** A light jacket for a patio table, since late-October mornings in Sedona often start in the 40s and warm into the 70s by afternoon. Bring a water bottle you can refill, and your Red Rock Pass if you are heading straight to a trailhead afterward.
