Montezuma Castle National Monument is the best-preserved cliff dwelling within easy reach of Sedona: a five-story, 20-room Sinagua home tucked into a limestone cliff about 90 feet above Beaver Creek, seen from a flat, paved one-third-mile loop. Adults pay $10 for a pass that also covers Tuzigoot National Monument for seven days, kids under 16 are free, and the whole visit takes about an hour, which makes it the easiest ancient site in the Verde Valley to fold into a day that also includes wine.

We've been driving guests through the Verde Valley since 2004, and the castle is the stop people most often tell us they wish they'd known about sooner. Here's how to see it well, what else sits within a few miles of it, and how to build a full day around it.

## What you'll see at Montezuma Castle National Monument

The Sinagua people lived along Beaver Creek for more than 400 years, and [Visit Sedona](https://visitsedona.com/sedona-secret-7/ancient-trails/montezuma-castle-national-monument/) dates the dwelling's occupation to roughly 1100 to 1425 AD. The name is a famous mistake: early American settlers assumed the Aztec emperor Montezuma had something to do with it. He didn't. It was built and lived in by local farming families who planted along the creek below.

You won't go inside. Early visitors climbed ladders into the rooms, but public access ended in 1951 because of the damage it was causing. What you get instead is a remarkably intact view from the trail. The loop runs through a grove of big Arizona sycamores along the creek, and it's one of the better short bird-watching walks in the valley, especially in fall when the sycamores turn gold.

A few things worth doing while you're there:

- **Walk the full loop.** It's paved, mostly level and about a third of a mile, according to the [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/moca/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm), so it works for strollers and most mobility levels.
- **Catch a ranger program.** [Visit Arizona](https://www.visitarizona.com/like-a-local/your-guide-to-exploring-montezuma-castle-national-monument) says they usually run about 25 minutes, and they add a lot of context about daily life on Beaver Creek.
- **Spend ten minutes in the museum.** The visitor center displays artifacts recovered in the area and covers both the castle and Montezuma Well.
- **Picnic by the creek.** It's shady, quiet on weekday mornings and a nice reset before an afternoon of tasting.

## Hours, fees and getting there

Here's what we confirmed this week. Park hours do shift around holidays, so check the current hours on the [NPS site](https://home.nps.gov/moca/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm) or call the monument at (928) 567-3322 before a holiday visit.

- **Hours:** The park, visitor center and trails are open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. daily, per [Visit Arizona](https://www.visitarizona.com/like-a-local/your-guide-to-exploring-montezuma-castle-national-monument). Expect early closures on Thanksgiving and Christmas Eve, and closures on Christmas Day and New Year's Day.
- **Fees:** $10 per adult 16 and older, good for seven days at both Montezuma Castle and Tuzigoot. Children under 16 are free.
- **Getting there:** Take Interstate 17 to exit 289, head east through two traffic circles for about half a mile, and turn left on Montezuma Castle Road at the blinking red light.
- **From Sedona:** Take State Route 179 south through the Village of Oak Creek to I-17, then go south to exit 289. Plan on 35 to 45 minutes from Uptown, less from the Village of Oak Creek.

Fall and spring weekends get busy by late morning, so an opening-hour arrival makes the whole day smoother.

## Montezuma Well: the stop most people skip

[Montezuma Well](https://nps.gov/moca/planyourvisit/exploring-montezuma-well.htm) is a separate unit of the same monument about 11 miles away, roughly 20 minutes by car. From the castle, drive north on I-17 four miles to exit 293, then follow signs through McGuireville and Rimrock for about four more miles.

It's a limestone sinkhole fed by underground springs. The Park Service says it takes in about 1.5 million gallons of water every day and holds more than 15 million gallons, in a place that gets less than 13 inches of rain a year. That water has made it an oasis for a very long time. The Sinagua built more than 30 rooms along the rim by the 1100s, and two small cliff dwellings still sit in a natural ledge on the cliff face.

The best part is the walk down to the outlet. Water leaves the Well through a narrow cave in the southeast rim, called the swallet, and emerges at the outlet, where it flows into a water channel along the creek. The NPS notes the outlet trail can be up to 20 degrees cooler than the rim thanks to the shade, which is welcome on a warm afternoon. The water itself is home to species found nowhere else, including a springsnail only two to three millimeters long.

Give the Well 45 minutes to an hour. Most visitors who skip it do so because they didn't realize it was part of the same monument, so this is your tip.

## Tuzigoot National Monument and the Crane Petroglyph Heritage Site

Your castle pass also covers [Tuzigoot National Monument](https://visitsedona.com/sedona-secret-7/ancient-trails/tuzigoot-national-monument/) in Clarkdale, a hilltop Sinagua pueblo built around 1100 CE with 110 rooms and up to three stories. Unlike the castle, the trail takes you right up to and around the pueblo walls. The trail is short, about a quarter mile, but steep in places. Visit Sedona lists the hours as 8 a.m. to 4:45 p.m. daily; check the current hours before you go.

Tuzigoot overlooks Tavasci Marsh and the Verde River, and it sits just outside Cottonwood, so it pairs naturally with an afternoon of tasting in Old Town. The marsh area has had fire-related closures in recent years, so check current trail conditions with the Park Service before planning a marsh walk.

The third great site is on Forest Service land. The [Crane Petroglyph Heritage Site](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r03/coconino/recreation/crane-petroglyph-heritage-site), formerly known as the V Bar V Heritage Site, is the largest known petroglyph site in the Verde Valley, with rock art in the Beaver Creek style associated with the Southern Sinagua. It's 2.8 miles east of the I-17 and SR 179 junction on Forest Road 618, just past the Beaver Creek Day-Use Area, which makes it the closest of the four sites to Sedona.

Know its schedule before you go, because it trips people up:

- Open Friday through Monday only.
- October through May: 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m., with the entrance gate closing at 2:30 p.m.
- Closed Thanksgiving and Christmas, and whenever temperatures top 100 degrees.
- A Red Rock Pass ($5 a day, sold at the on-site machine) or an America the Beautiful pass is required to park. Our [Red Rock Pass guide](/blog/red-rock-pass-sedona) covers where else you'll need one.
- Volunteer guides are on site during open hours, and they're excellent.

## Can you bring your dog?

Partly. At Montezuma Castle and Montezuma Well, the [Park Service allows leashed pets](https://www.nps.gov/moca/planyourvisit/pets.htm) on established trails, paved roads, picnic areas and parking lots, on a leash no longer than six feet. Pets aren't allowed in visitor centers or restrooms, and you should never leave a dog in the car, even on a mild fall day. Both monuments also run a B.A.R.K. Ranger program if your pup wants a badge.

The Crane Petroglyph Heritage Site does not allow pets, so if your dog is along, swap that stop for a second walk at the Well.

## A half-day loop from Sedona, with wine

Because the four sites run in a neat line along I-17 and the Verde River, they're easy to link without backtracking. Here's the order we suggest for a Friday through Monday, when the petroglyph site is open:

1. **9:30 a.m., Crane Petroglyph Heritage Site.** Closest to Sedona and the earliest gate. Allow an hour.
2. **11 a.m., Montezuma Well.** Back out to I-17 and exit 293. Allow 45 minutes.
3. **Noon, Montezuma Castle.** South to exit 289. Picnic by the creek or grab lunch in Camp Verde.
4. **Afternoon, wine.** You're already in wine country. [Visit Camp Verde](https://visitcampverde.com/wine-tasting/) lists Clear Creek Vineyard & Winery on State Route 260, Salt Mine Wine (tastings Saturdays and holidays) and Alcantara Vineyards on the Verde River near Cornville. Page Springs Road and Old Town Cottonwood are each a short drive from there; our guides to [Page Springs wine tasting](/blog/page-springs-wine-tasting) and [Old Town Cottonwood](/blog/old-town-cottonwood-wine-tasting) cover those stops.

If you'd rather skip the driving once the tasting starts, that's exactly what we do. [Wine Tours of Sedona](/wine-tours-of-sedona) and our sister brands pick you up in Sedona and handle the Verde Valley so you can enjoy every pour, and our [Sedona wineries and vineyards](/sedona-wineries-and-vineyards) page lays out the rest of the valley. A morning on your own at the castle followed by a guided afternoon of tasting is one of the best days in Arizona.

For a museum add-on, the [Verde Valley Archaeology Center & Museum](https://www.verdevalleyarchaeology.org/visit) at 460 W. Finnie Flat Road in Camp Verde is open Tuesday through Saturday from 10 a.m. (last admission 3 p.m.). Adults are $10 and kids under 18 are free.

## What to bring, when to go and what to book

**When to go.** Late October through November is prime time: cool mornings, sycamores turning along Beaver Creek, and warm, comfortable afternoons. Winter is quiet and pleasant, though it can be chilly in the shade at the Well. Summer works if you go at opening, and note that the petroglyph site switches to 8 a.m.-to-noon hours from June through September.

**What to bring:**

- Water and a hat, even in fall; the castle loop has shade but the Well's rim doesn't.
- Binoculars for the castle's upper rooms and the birds along the creek.
- Comfortable shoes for the Well's outlet trail and Tuzigoot's steeper path.
- A Red Rock Pass or America the Beautiful pass if you're visiting the petroglyphs.
- Cash or a card for the $10 castle and Tuzigoot entry; keep the receipt, since it covers both for seven days.
