A Sedona to Grand Canyon day trip is very doable: the South Rim is about 110 miles from Uptown Sedona, and the drive takes roughly two to two and a quarter hours each way. Leave by 7 a.m., spend five or six hours on the rim, and you are back in Sedona for a late dinner. This fall there are a few things to know before you go, including water restrictions in the park and several closed restaurants and lodges, so we have put the current details in one place.

We have been guiding in Sedona since 2004, and "how far is the Grand Canyon from Sedona?" is one of the questions we hear most. Here is how we would plan the day ourselves.

## How Far Is the Grand Canyon from Sedona?

The answer depends on which rim and which entrance you mean. For a day trip, you want the South Rim, which is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

- **Sedona to Grand Canyon Village (South Entrance):** about 110 miles, 2 to 2.25 hours via Flagstaff and US-180.
- **Sedona to Desert View (East Entrance):** about 110 miles, roughly 2.25 hours via Flagstaff, US-89 and Cameron.
- **Sedona to the North Rim:** well over 200 miles and about four hours each way. That is not a day trip, and the North Rim's 2026 season wraps up on October 15 anyway, with no water available there this year.

The National Park Service lists the South Rim at 81 miles from Flagstaff via US-180 and 63 miles from Williams via AZ-64 on its [directions page](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/directions_s_rim.htm). Sedona to Flagstaff adds about 29 miles and 45 to 50 minutes up Oak Creek Canyon on AZ-89A.

## The Best Route for a Sedona to Grand Canyon Day Trip

There are three practical ways to drive it, and the one we recommend uses two of them.

**Route 1: Oak Creek Canyon and US-180 (the direct way).** Take AZ-89A north through Oak Creek Canyon to Flagstaff, then US-180 northwest past the San Francisco Peaks to AZ-64 at Valle, and north to the South Entrance near Tusayan. It is the shortest route and a beautiful one, especially in October when the aspens near the Peaks and the maples in Oak Creek Canyon are turning. The switchbacks at the top of Oak Creek Canyon are slow, and the road is two lanes most of the way.

**Route 2: I-17, I-40 and Williams (the easy way).** From the Village of Oak Creek, take AZ-179 to I-17 north, I-40 west to Williams, and AZ-64 north. It is closer to 150 miles and about two and a half hours, but it is mostly interstate. We use it when it is dark, when there is snow or ice on US-180 or in Oak Creek Canyon, or when someone in the car does not love switchbacks.

**Route 3: US-89 through Cameron (the east way).** From Flagstaff, take US-89 north about 50 miles to Cameron, then AZ-64 west about 30 miles to the East Entrance at Desert View. You climb out of the Painted Desert with the Little Colorado River Gorge beside you, and your first look at the canyon is from the Desert View Watchtower.

**Our pick is the loop:** go in through the east at Desert View in the morning, drive the 23-mile Desert View Drive west to Grand Canyon Village, and come home on US-180. The East Entrance almost never has the line that the South Entrance does, you are never backtracking, and the whole loop is about 245 miles and five and a half hours of driving. Before you leave, check [AZ511](https://www.az511.gov/) for road work or weather closures.

One quirk on the east route: Cameron is on the Navajo Nation, which observes daylight saving time while the rest of Arizona does not. Until clocks change on November 1, your phone may jump ahead an hour near Cameron. The park itself stays on Arizona time.

## What Is Open and Closed at the South Rim This Fall

This is the part that changes, so we checked the park's [operations update](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/grand-canyon-national-park-public-health-update.htm), last revised October 4, 2026. Please look at it again the day before you go.

- **No reservation or timed entry is needed** to enter the park.
- **Stage 4 water restrictions are in effect.** Bring your own drinking water for the day rather than counting on filling stations.
- **Dining is limited.** The park lists the Bright Angel Lodge coffee bar, Harvey House Cafe and the Arizona Steakhouse (dinner only) as open. El Tovar's dining room and the Maswik Food Court are listed as closed, and Yavapai Lodge is closed during the water restrictions.
- **The South Rim Visitor Center** is open 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., moving to 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on October 12. Yavapai Geology Museum, Kolb Studio and Verkamp's are listed at 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.
- **Desert View Watchtower** is open 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., with the upper floors open when staffed and last access around 5 p.m. Admission is free.
- **Below the rim,** Phantom Ranch and the North Kaibab Trail are closed after flash flooding on August 29. That mostly affects overnight hikers, but if you plan to walk partway down a trail, read the park's current trail notices first.

None of this should talk you out of going. The views are exactly where they have always been. It just means this is the year to pack a cooler.

## Grand Canyon Entrance Fees for 2026

Per the park's [fees page](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/fees.htm):

- **$35 per private vehicle,** good for seven days and everyone in the car.
- **$30 per motorcycle,** $20 per person on foot or bicycle.
- **$80 for the America the Beautiful annual pass** (U.S. residents), which pays for itself if you visit three national parks in a year. Seniors pay $20 for an annual pass.
- **International visitors:** new in 2026, each non-U.S. resident age 16 and older pays a $100 nonresident fee on top of the standard entrance fee. The non-resident annual pass is $250, so do the math for your group before you reach the gate.
- **The park does not accept cash.** Bring a credit or debit card.

## A Sample One-Day Itinerary from Sedona

Here is the day the way we would run it in October and early November, when sunset at the rim is between about 5:30 and 6 p.m.

**6:45 a.m.** Leave Sedona with coffee and a full tank. Head up Oak Creek Canyon while the light is coming over the rim.

**7:45 a.m.** Flagstaff. Top off the gas or grab breakfast downtown, then take US-89 north.

**9:00 a.m.** Cameron. The [Cameron Trading Post](https://www.camerontradingpost.com/) has been here since 1916 and is a good stop for a Navajo taco, restrooms and a walk through the gallery.

**10:15 a.m.** Desert View. Climb the Watchtower, then start west on Desert View Drive. The park's [Desert View page](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/desert-view.htm) describes the stops; our favorites are Navajo Point, Lipan Point for the long view of the Colorado River, Moran Point and Grandview Point. Tusayan Pueblo, a small ancestral Puebloan site, is a quick and worthwhile stop along the way.

**12:30 p.m.** Picnic lunch. With park dining limited this fall, a cooler is the easy answer.

**1:30 p.m.** Park at the Visitor Center and walk to Mather Point, then follow the paved Rim Trail west toward Yavapai Geology Museum, about 0.7 mile. Keep going to the Village if you feel like it; the trail is mostly flat and the free shuttle will bring you back.

**3:00 p.m.** Ride the free Hermit Road (Red) shuttle from the Village. Hermit Road is closed to private vehicles through November 30, and the buses run about every 12 minutes according to the park's [shuttle page](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/shuttle-buses.htm). Hopi Point and Mohave Point are the classic sunset stops. If you would rather not ride a bus back in the dark, catch the last light from Yavapai Point or Mather Point instead.

**6:15 p.m.** Head south on AZ-64 and US-180, or take I-40 and I-17 if you prefer interstate at night. Watch for elk and deer, which are everywhere along these roads after dark. You will be in Sedona around 8:30 p.m.

## What to Book, What to Bring and When to Go

**What to book:** nothing, for a day trip. There is no entry reservation. If you decide to stay the night instead, in-park lodging is tighter than usual this fall, so look at Tusayan, Williams or Flagstaff as well.

**What to bring:**

- At least two to three quarts of water per person, plus lunch and snacks
- Layers. The South Rim sits at about 7,000 feet, roughly 2,500 feet higher than Sedona, so plan on it being 10 to 15 degrees cooler, with mornings near freezing by late October
- A credit or debit card for the entrance fee, or your annual pass and photo ID
- Sun protection and real walking shoes, even for the paved Rim Trail
- A leash if the dog is coming. Leashed pets are welcome on the trails above the rim, including the Rim Trail, but not below the rim or on the shuttle buses

**When to go:** weekdays are noticeably quieter than weekends. If you use the South Entrance, arrive before 9 a.m. or after mid-afternoon to miss the line. October through mid-November is one of our favorite windows: cool air, long views and far fewer people than summer. From December on, check the [park's road conditions](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/weather-condition.htm) for snow before you leave.

## Is the Day Trip Worth It?

Yes, with one honest caveat: it is a long day, with four and a half to five and a half hours in the car. If you only have two days in Sedona, we would spend both of them here among the red rocks. With three or more, the Grand Canyon is absolutely worth one of them. We covered how to pace a fall visit in [The Best Time to Visit Sedona? October Makes a Strong Case](/blog/best-time-to-visit-sedona-fall) and in our guide to [Sedona in November](/blog/sedona-in-november).
