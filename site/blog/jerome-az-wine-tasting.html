Jerome AZ Wine Tasting: A Guide's Plan for the Hilltop Tasting Rooms

Jerome AZ wine tasting from a Sedona guide since 2004: which tasting rooms to visit, current hours, parking, the drive from Sedona and how to plan the day.

By Jim Reich · November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Jerome AZ wine tasting is one of the most fun ways to taste Arizona wine: a handful of tasting rooms sit within a few blocks of each other on the side of Cleopatra Hill, about 30 miles from Sedona, so you can park once and walk between pours. Start at Caduceus Cellars on Main Street, add one or two more rooms on Main and Hull Avenue, and leave time for lunch with a view across the whole Verde Valley.

We have been bringing guests to Jerome since 2004, back when there were far fewer places to pour a glass. Here is how we would plan a tasting day there now, with the current hours we could confirm and the practical details that make it easy.

Why Jerome is a great place for wine tasting

Most of the Verde Valley’s wine is spread out: vineyards along Page Springs Road, tasting rooms in Old Town Cottonwood, wineries tucked into Cornville. Jerome is different. The tasting rooms are clustered in an old copper mining town that was home to nearly 15,000 people at its peak, according to the Town of Jerome, and now has only a few hundred residents. You taste inside century-old brick buildings, step outside, and the view runs all the way across the valley to the red rocks of Sedona.

Jerome is also a front door to the Verde Valley American Viticultural Area, the federally recognized wine region that, as Caduceus Cellars notes, was designated in 2021. The wines poured in town are made from grapes grown down the hill in the Verde Valley and, for some producers, in southeast Arizona around Willcox. If you are new to all of this, our first-timer’s guide to Arizona wine explains the grapes and labels you will see.

Jerome tasting rooms we send guests to

Hours change with the season, and small tasting rooms sometimes close early on slow weekdays, so treat the hours below as a starting point and check before you go.

Caduceus Cellars and Merkin Vineyards

The anchor of Jerome wine tasting is the Caduceus Cellars tasting room at 158 Main St., the home of Caduceus and Merkin Vineyards wines, with tasting flights and a bottle shop. Caduceus lists its Jerome hours as 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday through Thursday and 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. on Friday and Saturday. Visit Arizona also mentions espresso drinks from 8 a.m. to noon and a small “Merkin Marketplace” of imported and local foods, which is handy if you arrive early.

What makes this room interesting is the range of vineyards behind it. Caduceus describes about 110 acres under vine at elevations from 3,300 to 4,900 feet, with sites near Cornville, on Oak Creek, in Old Town Cottonwood, at Yavapai College’s campus in Clarkdale and in the Willcox area. Expect a lot of Italian and Spanish grapes such as Sangiovese, Nebbiolo, Aglianico, Tempranillo and Garnacha, plus whites like Malvasia Bianca and Albariño. Ask the staff which vineyard each wine comes from; it is the best quick lesson in Verde Valley terroir you will get anywhere.

Weekends get busy, especially Saturday afternoons. If the room is packed, browse the bottle shop, walk Main Street and come back in half an hour.

Cellar 433

On Hull Avenue, one level below Main Street, Cellar 433 at 240 Hull Ave. pours Arizona wines grown, produced and bottled by winemaker and grower John McLoughlin of Willcox, under the banner “AZ grapes = AZ Wine.” The Cottonwood Chamber lists hours of 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Thursday and 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. Friday through Sunday, and the space is also available for private events. Note the earlier closing time on weekends, which surprises a lot of people.

Hull Avenue is a one-way street, so if you are driving in, follow the signs and plan to park and walk.

Passion Cellars

Passion Cellars also has a Jerome tasting room, where, in its own words, the room “provides a perfect view into the barrel room” and you can taste and buy all of its wines. We could not confirm current hours or tasting prices for this room, so check directly before you build your day around it.

A note on pacing

Jerome has a few other places to drink wine, including wine bars and restaurants with Arizona wine lists, and the lineup shifts over the years. Our advice is to pick two or three tasting rooms, not five. Two flights plus lunch makes a full, relaxed afternoon. Our wine tasting in Sedona guide has typical tasting costs across the region if you want to budget.

Getting from Sedona to Jerome

Take State Route 89A southwest out of West Sedona toward Cottonwood, follow it through Clarkdale and climb the switchbacks into Jerome. Figure about 30 miles and 45 minutes to an hour, depending on traffic in Cottonwood. The last stretch is steep with tight hairpin turns, and Jerome’s streets stack on top of each other, so take it slow and watch for pedestrians stepping off the sidewalks.

Here is the honest part: the drive down from Jerome after a few flights is exactly where a designated driver matters most. Those switchbacks deserve your full attention, and the whole point of a wine day is that everyone gets to enjoy it. That is the day our guides drive every week.

Parking in Jerome

The Town of Jerome runs pay-to-park zones in town, including the lot near the Sliding Jail, the Hull Avenue lot and the east side of Main Street. When we last checked, parking in those zones was $5 for the whole day, charged from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. and paid by card at the kiosks, with free parking for vehicles with disability placards. There is also a free lot on Perkinsville Road, and the town has run a free shuttle on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and holidays. Check the town’s page for current rates and shuttle days.

On busy weekends, arrive before 11 a.m. The lots near Main Street fill quickly, and the walk up from lower lots is short but steep.

How to plan a Jerome wine tasting day

Here is the half-day we would build for friends visiting Sedona:

  1. 9:30 a.m. Leave Sedona on 89A.
  2. 10:30 a.m. Park, then walk up to Jerome State Historic Park and the Douglas Mansion for the mining history and the big view. Check current hours and admission before you go.
  3. 11:30 a.m. First tasting at Caduceus Cellars on Main Street.
  4. 12:30 p.m. Lunch on Main Street. Eat before your second flight, not after.
  5. 1:45 p.m. Second tasting at Cellar 433 on Hull Avenue, which is easy to reach on foot.
  6. 3 p.m. Galleries and shops, then head down the hill, or continue to Old Town Cottonwood’s tasting rooms about 15 minutes away.

For a full Verde Valley day, Jerome pairs well with Cottonwood in the afternoon, and the creekside wineries of Page Springs are a bit farther, on the Sedona side of the valley. Our Verde Valley wine trail map lays out the drive times between each area, and our Jerome day trip guide covers the museums, the Audrey Headframe and the best places to eat.

What to book, what to bring, when to go

When to go. Weekdays are the calmest, and late morning is the sweet spot before the lunch crowd. Fall and spring are ideal, with cool afternoons and clear views across the valley. Jerome sits at more than 5,000 feet, so winter can bring wind, cold and the occasional dusting of snow; check the forecast and road conditions on 89A before you go. Summer afternoons are warm but more comfortable than the valley floor.

What to book.

  • Your driver first. If you want to taste in Jerome and keep going into wine country, a tour takes care of the switchbacks, parking and timing. Wine Tours of Sedona runs private, all-inclusive wine days with Sedona pickup, and well-behaved dogs are welcome; reach Wine Tours of Sedona at (928) 224-2991 or www.winetoursofsedona.com, or read more on our Wine Tours of Sedona page.
  • Lunch reservations on weekends, if your restaurant takes them.
  • Any private or seated tasting experiences in advance; walk-in flights are usually first come, first served.

What to bring.

  • Comfortable shoes with good grip. Jerome is all hills and stairs.
  • A layer or windbreaker for the afternoon breeze.
  • Photo ID for every tasting room.
  • Water and snacks for between flights.
  • A card for the parking kiosks, plus a plan for carrying bottles home. A small cooler in the car keeps wine happy on warm days.
