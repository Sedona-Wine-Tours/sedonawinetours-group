Prescott Day Trip From Sedona: Whiskey Row, Watson Lake and the Drive

Plan a Prescott day trip from Sedona: the Mingus Mountain drive, Whiskey Row, Watson Lake fees and hours, Sharlot Hall Museum and 2026 Christmas City dates.

By Jim Reich · October 21, 2026 · 7 min read

A Prescott day trip from Sedona is one of the easiest and most rewarding drives in central Arizona: about 60 miles and roughly an hour and a half each way over Mingus Mountain on State Route 89A, through Cottonwood and Jerome. Give it a full day and you get a historic courthouse square, Whiskey Row, the granite boulders of Watson Lake and a cool-pines change of scenery, with wine country waiting on the drive home.

We have been guiding in the Verde Valley since 2004, and Prescott is the town we send people to when they ask, “What’s a real Arizona town that isn’t built around red rocks?” Here is how we would plan it.

The Sedona to Prescott drive: route, time and what to expect

The classic route is simple. Take 89A southwest out of West Sedona to Cottonwood, continue through Clarkdale and up the switchbacks into Jerome, then over Mingus Mountain and down into Prescott Valley, where 89A meets Highway 89 for the last few miles into downtown Prescott. By the highway mileposts it is just under 60 miles from the 89A/179 junction in Sedona to the southern end of 89A at Prescott. Plan on about 90 minutes without stops, and more like two hours once you pull over for the views.

The section that deserves your attention is the Mingus Mountain Scenic Road, an 11.5-mile stretch from Jerome up and over the mountain. According to ADOT’s corridor plan, it is a two-lane road with no standard shoulder, plenty of guardrail, some pullouts and many switchbacks, climbing toward a mountain that tops out around 7,800 feet. It is beautiful, it is well maintained, and it is not the place to hurry. If anyone in your group gets carsick, give them the front seat.

A few practical notes on the Sedona to Prescott drive:

  • Fill up in Cottonwood. Services thin out between Jerome and Prescott Valley.
  • Watch the weather from December through March. Snow and ice can hit Mingus when Sedona is simply chilly, and ADOT posts message signs on this segment in severe weather. Check AZ511 before you go.
  • The gentler alternative. If the mountain is icy or you are towing, take 179 south to I-17, then I-17 south to State Route 169 and west toward Prescott Valley. It is longer, but it is freeway and open highway rather than switchbacks.

We covered the Jerome stop in detail in our Jerome Arizona day trip guide, and it is worth a coffee break on the way up or a longer stop on the way back.

Whiskey Row and the courthouse square

Downtown Prescott centers on the Yavapai County Courthouse and its tree-shaded plaza, and the block of Montezuma Street facing it is Whiskey Row. This is the heart of any day in Prescott, and the best way to see it is on foot.

The history is the draw. On July 14, 1900, the Great Fire swept through downtown and destroyed most of Whiskey Row, as True West Magazine recounts. The town rebuilt quickly, and many of the brick storefronts you walk past today date from that rebuilding. The Palace, which first opened in 1877, is the saloon everyone asks about; its back bar and Old West stories make it the obvious stop for a sandwich or a cold drink.

How we would spend two hours downtown:

  • Walk the full loop of the courthouse plaza, then work your way down Whiskey Row and onto Cortez Street for antiques and vintage shops.
  • Have lunch on or near the Row. Prescott is a real lunch town, and you will find everything from burgers to bistro plates within a few blocks.
  • Duck into the Hassayampa Inn lobby a block away for a look at a 1920s hotel that still feels like one.

Leashed dogs are a common sight on the plaza lawn, so if you are traveling with a four-legged companion, downtown is an easy, shady place to stretch everyone’s legs. Check each shop’s and restaurant’s own pet policy before you walk in.

Watson Lake and the Granite Dells

About four miles from downtown, Watson Lake is the picture most people have in their head when they think of Prescott: blue water wrapped in rounded, stacked granite boulders known as the Granite Dells. It is a great place for an easy walk, a picnic or an afternoon paddle.

The details from the City of Prescott:

  • Address: 3101 Watson Lake Park Rd., off Highway 89.
  • Hours: 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. through October 31; winter hours are 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. from November 1 through March 31.
  • Day-use parking: $5 per vehicle at the kiosk. The city’s parking pass page notes that parking at its lakes and on the Peavine and Constellation trails is free on Wednesdays.
  • Trails: The Peavine Trail, a flat former railroad grade, runs along the lake’s edge and is the easiest way to see the Dells without scrambling. The rock-hopping loop routes around the lake are a different animal, with route-finding and painted markers on granite, so wear real shoes.

A visit to Watson Lake can fill a whole morning. If you are short on time, the Peavine overlook gives you the classic view in about 30 minutes.

Sharlot Hall Museum and Prescott’s territorial history

Prescott was Arizona’s first territorial capital, and the Sharlot Hall Museum, a few blocks west of the plaza at 415 W. Gurley St., tells that story across a campus of historic buildings, including the log Governor’s Mansion. Plan on at least 90 minutes.

Current hours and admission, per the museum:

  • October through April: Tuesday to Saturday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.; Sunday, noon to 4 p.m.; closed Mondays.
  • Closed Thanksgiving, Christmas Day and New Year’s Day, and closing at 1 p.m. on Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve.
  • Admission: $17 adults, $15 seniors (65+) and military, $9 college students and youth 10 to 17, free for kids 9 and under. Tickets are good for two consecutive days.

Because the museum is closed on Mondays, Tuesday through Saturday is the best window for a Prescott day trip from Sedona if history is on your list.

Prescott’s Christmas City season: 2026 dates

Prescott calls itself Arizona’s Christmas City, and if you are visiting in the next six weeks, it is worth timing your trip around one of the big nights. Here are the 2026 dates listed by Experience Prescott:

  • Prescott Holiday Light Parade: Saturday, November 28, 6 to 8:30 p.m.
  • Gingerbread Village: daily from November 28 through January 1, 2027, at Prescott Resort and Conference Center.
  • 44th Annual Christmas Parade: Saturday, December 5, 1 to 3 p.m.
  • Frontier Christmas at Sharlot Hall Museum: Saturday, December 5, 3 to 8 p.m.
  • Courthouse Lighting: Saturday, December 5, 5 to 7:30 p.m.
  • Acker Musical Showcase: Friday, December 11, 5 to 8:30 p.m.
  • Whiskey Row New Year’s Eve Boot Drop: December 31, starting at 7 p.m.

December 5 is the big one: the parade, the museum’s Frontier Christmas and the courthouse lighting all happen the same day, and downtown gets crowded. Arrive by late morning, park once and stay put. Event times can change, so check the official listing the week of your trip. And if you go on a winter evening, remember you will be driving Mingus Mountain home in the dark, so leave a little earlier than you think you need to, or book a room in Prescott for the night.

When to go, what to book and what to bring

When to go. Late October through November is prime: cool, clear days, fall color in the cottonwoods along the Verde Valley and fewer summer crowds. Prescott sits higher and cooler than Sedona, so expect noticeably cooler air than in Sedona, especially in the shade and after sunset. December brings the holiday events and a real chance of snow on the mountain.

What to book.

  • Dinner on December 5 or New Year’s Eve. Downtown restaurants fill early on event days, so reserve ahead if you want a table.
  • A room, if you want the evening. If the courthouse lighting or the Boot Drop is your reason for going, staying overnight in Prescott takes the night drive over Mingus off the table.
  • Your wine day. If you want to taste on the way home through Jerome and Cottonwood, or on a separate day, let someone else do the driving. Our Sedona wineries and vineyards guide lays out the options, and our walkable Old Town Cottonwood wine tasting guide covers the town you will drive straight through.

What to bring.

  • A warm layer and a jacket for after dark, even if Sedona felt like shirtsleeve weather.
  • Real walking shoes for the Watson Lake granite.
  • Water and snacks for the drive, and $5 cash or a card for lake parking (free on Wednesdays).
  • A cooler bag if you plan to bring home bottles from the Verde Valley.

A suggested day: leave Sedona by 8:30 a.m., coffee in Jerome, Watson Lake by 10:30, lunch on Whiskey Row, Sharlot Hall Museum in the afternoon, and back over Mingus before dark, with a stop in Cottonwood for an early dinner.
