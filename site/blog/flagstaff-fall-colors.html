Flagstaff Fall Colors 2026: Where to See the Aspens Right Now

Flagstaff fall colors peak in early to mid October. Where to see the aspens in 2026 with Lockett Meadow closed, plus the easy day trip up from Sedona.

By Jim Reich · October 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Flagstaff fall colors peak in the first half of October, which means the aspens on the San Francisco Peaks are turning gold right now. This year comes with one big change: the road to Lockett Meadow, the most famous aspen spot in Arizona, is closed for the whole season, so the best viewing in 2026 is on the west side of the Peaks along Snowbowl Road and Hart Prairie. From Sedona it is about 30 miles and 45 minutes to an hour up Oak Creek Canyon, which makes this one of the easiest day trips we know.

We have been driving guests around northern Arizona since 2004, and the question we hear every October is some version of “where do we go to see the leaves?” Here is how we would plan it this year.

When Flagstaff fall colors peak in 2026

Color in northern Arizona moves downhill. The Coconino National Forest says fall color begins to appear in late September and peaks through October, with the higher elevations turning first and Flagstaff’s high country reaching its prime in early October. Oak Creek Canyon, a few thousand feet lower, holds its color well into November.

In practical terms, for this year:

  • Now through about mid-October: aspens on the San Francisco Peaks, from roughly 8,000 feet up to treeline. This is the window for Snowbowl Road, Hart Prairie and the high trails.
  • Mid to late October: color drops toward town (Flagstaff sits near 7,000 feet) and into the upper end of Oak Creek Canyon.
  • Late October into mid-November: Oak Creek Canyon, Sedona and then the cottonwoods along the Verde River.

A hard wind or an early snow can strip the high aspens in a day, so check before you drive. Discover Flagstaff runs a weekly LEAF-ometer during the season, and the Forest Service links to it from its own fall color page.

What is closed this fall: Lockett Meadow and West Fork

Two of the best-known fall color spots in the region are off the table this October, and it is worth knowing before you load the car.

Lockett Meadow and the Inner Basin. The Forest Service lists Lockett Meadow Road (Forest Road 552) as closed through November 2026 while heavy machinery does maintenance work. The closure covers all traffic, including people on foot and on bikes, so walking up the road is not an option. If you had the Inner Basin Trail on your list, save it for next year.

West Fork of Oak Creek. After this summer’s Pocket Fire, Oak Creek and most of its recreation sites reopened on September 17, but the West Fork watershed burned harder and still carries a flood and rockfall risk. The current closure order for the West Fork drainage and West Fork Trail No. 108 runs through October 31, 2026, and the Forest Service’s fall color page lists the Call of the Canyon picnic site, where that trail starts, as closed. It has already been extended once, so please check the alert page rather than assume it reopens November 1.

The good news is that neither closure touches the scenic drives, and there is plenty of gold left to see.

Where to see the aspens instead

These are all on the Forest Service’s own list of fall color spots in the Flagstaff Ranger District, and all are on the west and north sides of the Peaks.

Snowbowl Road and the Aspen Nature Loop

From downtown Flagstaff, take US 180 northwest about 7 miles and turn right on Snowbowl Road. The paved road climbs another 7 miles through ponderosa pine into thick stands of aspen and ends above 9,000 feet. Any passenger car can do it.

Near the top, the Aspen Nature Loop is the walk we send most people on: a gentle lollipop of roughly 2.5 miles through aspen groves and open meadows with long views west toward Kendrick Peak. It starts well above 9,000 feet, so take it slow if you drove up from Phoenix that morning.

The Arizona Snowbowl gondola

If walking at altitude is not for you, the scenic gondola at Arizona Snowbowl does the climbing. For fall 2026 it is running daily from 10 AM to 4 PM through October 18, weather permitting, and tops out at 11,500 feet with the aspen canopy laid out below you. AZ Family reported that advance tickets start at $11 on select dates and that sunset rides run on Friday and Saturday evenings. Prices move with the date, so buy online ahead of time and confirm the hours the morning you go.

Hart Prairie Road

Hart Prairie Road (Forest Road 151) leaves US 180 about 10 miles northwest of Flagstaff and loops back to the highway farther north. It is a graded gravel road through big meadows ringed with aspen, with the Peaks filling the windshield. In dry weather a regular car driven slowly is usually fine; after rain or snow it gets rutted and slick. Look at the forest roads status page before you commit.

Kendrick Park Watchable Wildlife Trail

About 20 miles northwest of Flagstaff on US 180, this is the easiest stop on the list: a short paved loop of about a quarter mile and a longer dirt loop of about a mile and a half, both nearly flat, through aspen and pine at the edge of a wide meadow. It is a good choice for strollers, wheelchairs on the paved loop, and anyone who wants color without a climb.

For hikers and long-drive people

The Forest Service also lists the Weatherford and Abineau trails, the Schultz Pass scenic drive, and the Around the Peaks loop, which circles the mountain on a mix of highway and forest road. Abineau is steep and high, and the forest roads on the north side are rougher than Hart Prairie. These are for people with a full day, a capable vehicle and a paper map.

The Sedona to Flagstaff fall color day trip

Here is the day we would put together from Sedona.

Leave by 8 AM and drive State Route 89A north through Oak Creek Canyon. The canyon itself will be mostly green in early October, but the drive up the switchbacks to the Oak Creek Vista overlook is worth it in any season. You are in Flagstaff in under an hour.

Go straight to Snowbowl Road before the midday crowd. Walk the Aspen Nature Loop or ride the gondola, then continue up US 180 to Kendrick Park, and come back by way of Hart Prairie Road if conditions are dry. Have a late lunch in downtown Flagstaff, and be back in Sedona by late afternoon.

Weekends in early October are busy, and parking at the top of Snowbowl Road fills. A weekday, or an arrival before 9:30, makes the whole day easier.

What to bring and when to go

  • Layers. Flagstaff is 2,500 feet higher than Sedona and the aspens are higher still. A 78-degree afternoon in Sedona can be 50 and breezy at the top of Snowbowl Road, and mornings start near freezing.
  • Water and sun protection. The air is thin and dry at 9,000 feet even when it is cold.
  • A full tank and an offline map. Cell coverage drops quickly on the forest roads north of the Peaks.
  • A leash. Dogs are welcome on Coconino National Forest trails on a leash, and the Aspen Nature Loop and Kendrick Park are both easy on four legs. Check with Snowbowl directly on the gondola before you bring a dog up.
  • A current conditions check. The LEAF-ometer for color, the forest roads status page for gravel roads, and Snowbowl’s hours page for the gondola.

Best timing: this week and next for the high aspens. If you cannot come until late October, skip the Peaks and plan on Oak Creek Canyon and Sedona instead. We covered that stretch of the season in why October makes a strong case for Sedona and in our guide to Sedona in November.

After the aspens: fall in Sedona wine country

The nice thing about fall color in northern Arizona is that it does not end when the aspens drop. The gold works its way down Oak Creek Canyon through late October, and by November the cottonwoods along Oak Creek and the Verde River are lit up around Page Springs and Cottonwood, right where the Verde Valley’s wineries and vineyards are. Harvest is wrapping up, the patios are comfortable at midday, and the crowds thin out after Halloween.

A lot of our guests pair the two: a day in the high country with the aspens, then a day in the valley with someone else doing the driving. If that sounds like your kind of trip, the Tour Chooser will point you to the right one of our tours in about a minute.
