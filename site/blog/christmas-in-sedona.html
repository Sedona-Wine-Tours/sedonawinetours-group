Christmas in Sedona 2026: Tree Lighting, Luminarias and Holiday Events

Christmas in Sedona 2026 from a local guide: the Dec. 4 tree lighting, Breakfast with Santa, luminarias, holiday markets, what's open Dec. 25 and how to plan.

By Jim Reich · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Christmas in Sedona is quieter and warmer than most people expect: sunny afternoons in the high 50s, cold starry nights, luminarias in the courtyards at Tlaquepaque, and a calendar of small-town holiday events that kicks off with the free tree lighting on Friday, December 4, 2026. Christmas Day itself falls on a Friday this year, so the long holiday weekend will be busy; book lodging and dinner now, and plan your festive evenings around the first three weekends of December.

We have spent every December in red rock country since 2004, and this is the guide we give friends who ask what Christmas in Sedona is really like. Every 2026 date below comes from the organizer or an official calendar, and we link to each one so you can confirm before you go.

What Christmas in Sedona feels like

Sedona sits around 4,500 feet, so December is real winter without the misery. Normal highs run in the upper 50s and lows dip near freezing, and the sun sets around 5:20 p.m. That means bright, comfortable middays for a walk or a wine tasting, and genuinely cold evenings for lights and carols. Pack a warm coat, gloves and a hat for anything after dark.

Snow is possible but rarely lingers in town. When a storm does dust the red rocks, it is the most beautiful morning of the year, and it is usually gone from the roads by afternoon. Flagstaff, about 2,500 feet higher, is a different story, so check conditions before any drive north. One more 2026 note: State Route 89A through Oak Creek Canyon north of Slide Rock is closed after the November 1 flood, so the drive to Flagstaff runs via SR 179 and Interstate 17 for now. Our Oak Creek Canyon closure update has the details.

Sedona holiday events 2026: the calendar

These are the Sedona and Verde Valley events we could verify for this season, in date order.

Friday, December 4: Sedona Tree Lighting and Santa Visit. This is the official start of the season. Tlaquepaque lights its 26-foot Christmas tree at Tlaquepaque North from 5 to 6:30 p.m., Santa arrives, and there is complimentary hot chocolate and local holiday entertainment. Admission is free, per Visit Sedona’s event page. The event is co-sponsored by the Sedona Chamber of Commerce and Tourism Bureau. Arrive by 4:30 p.m. if you want a parking spot without circling.

December 4 to 6: Sedona Arts Center Holiday Artist Market. About 25 local artists and makers fill the Art Barn at 15 Art Barn Road in Uptown. The opening reception is Friday, December 4, from 4 to 6 p.m., then the market runs Saturday, December 5, from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Sunday, December 6, from 11 a.m. to 3 p.m., according to the Sedona Arts Center. It is the best place in town to find a gift that was actually made here, and it pairs neatly with the tree lighting the same evening.

Saturday, December 5: Breakfast with Santa. The City of Sedona’s Parks and Recreation team serves pancakes, juice and warm cocoa at the Hub at Posse Grounds Park from 8 to 10 a.m., with carolers, games and photos with Santa and his elves. A sensory-friendly session runs from 7:30 to 7:55 a.m. Details are on the City of Sedona’s event page (the web address says 2025, but the page lists the 2026 date).

Mid-December: Tlaquepaque Festival of Lights. This is the one locals circle on the calendar. For decades, Tlaquepaque has lit thousands of luminarias, the small paper-bag candles of Southwest tradition, through its courtyards and along its walls, usually with carolers and musicians and the bell tower ringing at dusk. It is traditionally held on a Saturday in mid-December. Tlaquepaque had not posted its 2026 date when we wrote this, so check tlaq.com or call the village at (928) 282-4838 before you plan around it. It is free to walk, it gets crowded, and the cold comes on fast once the sun drops, so dress for it.

Saturday, December 19: A Victorian Christmas at Fort Verde. Down in Camp Verde, Fort Verde State Historic Park hosts its Victorian Christmas from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m., with a tree lighting and carolers at dusk and the 1870s buildings lit with luminarias for touring until 8 p.m. There is no extra charge beyond regular park admission, per Arizona State Parks. It is about 35 minutes from Sedona and easy to pair with an afternoon at a Verde Valley winery.

All season: Red Rock Holidays at Enchantment. Enchantment Resort in Boynton Canyon lists complimentary skating daily from November 28 through January 31, 3 to 7 p.m., plus fireside wine and cocktail experiences. Check Enchantment’s Red Rock Holidays page for any guest-only restrictions before you drive out.

Christmas lights and festive day trips nearby

If you have a car and an extra evening, northern Arizona has more holiday sparkle within an hour or so. A 2026 statewide roundup from Raising Arizona Kids lists the Downtown Flagstaff holiday lights and tree lighting on December 5, 2026, and the Flagstaff Holiday Parade of Lights on December 12, 2026, both free. Remember that the Flagstaff drive currently goes via I-17, about an hour each way from Sedona, and that winter weather can close roads up high on short notice.

Closer in, Uptown Sedona and Tlaquepaque shops decorate for the whole month, and many West Sedona neighborhoods put on their own displays. Old Town Cottonwood and Jerome also dress up for the season and usually hold holiday events of their own; their 2026 dates were not posted yet when we checked, so look at the local chamber calendars before you go. Our Jerome wine tasting guide and Old Town Cottonwood guide cover the tasting rooms in both towns.

What’s open on Christmas Eve and Christmas Day

This is the question we get most often, and the honest answer is that it varies by business, so call ahead. A few things we can say for certain:

  • Montezuma Castle National Monument is closed on Christmas Day, per the National Park Service. Visit on the 24th or 26th instead.
  • Page Springs Cellars says on its FAQ page that it is open 365 days a year, which makes it the safest bet for a glass of wine on the holiday itself. Most other tasting rooms set their own holiday hours, and many close or close early on the 24th and 25th.
  • Restaurants. The resort dining rooms usually offer Christmas Eve and Christmas Day menus, and they fill up weeks ahead. Independent restaurants vary widely. Our guide to Sedona restaurants with a view is a good starting list; reserve as early as you can.
  • Trails and viewpoints are open, and Christmas morning is one of the quietest times of the year on the popular hikes. Many trailheads need a Red Rock Pass.

Wine country in December

December is one of our favorite months for wine tasting in the Verde Valley. Rooms are calm on weekdays, winemakers have time to talk, and the region’s bigger reds taste right on a cold afternoon. Bottles from Page Springs, Cornville, Cottonwood and Jerome also make good gifts to carry home, and many tasting rooms run wine clubs and holiday gift packs.

If you are bringing a group together, December is a natural time for it: family reunions over the holidays, office parties and friends’ weekends. A private wine tour takes the driving and the planning off everyone’s plate, and our groups, corporate and weddings page explains how we handle bigger parties. For first-timers, our Sedona wineries and vineyards guide lays out the three wine areas and how they differ.

What to book, what to bring, when to go

What to book now:

  • Lodging for Christmas week and New Year’s. Christmas Day falls on Friday, December 25, 2026, so the 24th through the 27th will be the busiest stretch of the month.
  • Christmas Eve and Christmas Day dinner reservations, especially at resorts.
  • Any wine tour or private tasting over the holiday weekend. Holiday dates go first, and some tasting rooms limit group sizes in December. You can reach us at Sedona Wine Tours, (928) 504-2445, tours@sedonawinetours.group, or www.sedonawinetours.group.

What to bring:

  • A warm coat, hat and gloves for evening events; temperatures can fall below freezing soon after sunset.
  • Layers for the day, sunglasses and sunscreen. The high-desert sun is strong even in December.
  • Traction devices if you plan to hike after a snow, and a printed map, since cell service is patchy on some trails.

When to go:

  • First weekend of December (Dec. 4 to 6) for the most festive energy: tree lighting, Holiday Artist Market and Breakfast with Santa in one trip.
  • Mid-December for the Festival of Lights and quieter lodging rates; confirm the date with Tlaquepaque first.
  • The week before Christmas for the Fort Verde Victorian Christmas on the 19th and easy restaurant reservations.
  • Christmas week if you want the full holiday buzz and do not mind crowds; book everything early.
