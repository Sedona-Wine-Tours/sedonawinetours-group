Verde Valley Wine Trail Map: Routes, Drive Times and a Day Planner

The Verde Valley Wine Trail map, explained by a local guide: the five tasting clusters, drive times from Sedona, and half-day, full-day and two-day routes.

By Jim Reich · October 29, 2026 · 8 min read

The Verde Valley Wine Trail map looks like a scatter of pins, but it really comes down to five clusters strung along two highways: in-town Sedona, the Page Springs and Cornville vineyards on Oak Creek, Old Town Cottonwood, Clarkdale, and Jerome on the hill, with Camp Verde’s estates off to the southeast. Pick two clusters for a day, three if you start early, and plan the order around drive times and closing hours rather than the map alone. Here is how we route it after more than two decades of driving these roads every day.

The official map lives on the Verde Valley Wine Trail site, which bills itself as the guide to the wines, vines and tasting rooms of Cottonwood, Sedona, Clarkdale and Jerome, and offers a printable version plus a Trail Passport and app for checking in at stops. It is a good overview. What it cannot tell you is which stops pair well, how long the drives really take, and which rooms are closed on the day you are going. That is what this planner is for.

How to read the Verde Valley Wine Trail map

Think of the valley as a rough triangle. Sedona sits at the northeast corner. State Route 89A runs southwest from Sedona to Cottonwood, then on through Clarkdale and up the switchbacks to Jerome. Page Springs Road drops south off 89A partway along, down to Oak Creek in Cornville. State Route 260 connects Cottonwood to Camp Verde and Interstate 17 on the southeast side.

The whole area is the Verde Valley American Viticultural Area, recognized in late 2021. Visit Sedona describes it as roughly 200 square miles of Yavapai County, mostly between 3,000 and 3,900 feet. That elevation is why the wine is here at all: warm days, cool nights and long growing seasons.

Two things the map does not show well:

  • Vineyards versus tasting rooms. The estate wineries with vines you can see are concentrated along Oak Creek in Page Springs and Cornville and along the Verde River near Camp Verde. Cottonwood, Clarkdale and Jerome are mostly tasting rooms in town, with walkable streets and restaurants next door.
  • Hills and switchbacks. Jerome looks close to Clarkdale on a flat map, and it is, but the last stretch of 89A climbs steeply with tight turns. Budget extra time and take it slow.

The five tasting clusters, with drive times from Sedona

These are our real-world estimates from Uptown Sedona in normal traffic. Saturday afternoons on 89A through Cottonwood can add 10 minutes or more.

1. In-town Sedona (0 to 10 minutes). No vineyards, but a handful of tasting rooms and wine bars pour Arizona wine near your hotel. Good for a first taste or a short evening. Our wine tasting in Sedona guide covers each one and what the flights cost.

2. Page Springs and Cornville (20 to 30 minutes). The creekside estates. Page Springs Cellars, Oak Creek Vineyards, Javelina Leap and DA Ranch sit close together along Oak Creek, and Cove Mesa Vineyard is a short drive away in Cornville. Page Springs Cellars at 1500 N. Page Springs Road is open 365 days a year, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday through Wednesday and until 8 p.m. Thursday through Saturday, with 90-minute reservations through Tock, per its FAQ page. DA Ranch is closed Monday and Tuesday. Our Page Springs wine tasting guide goes stop by stop.

3. Old Town Cottonwood (about 19 miles, 30 minutes). The walkable heart of the trail: around nine tasting rooms on a few flat blocks of Main Street, from Arizona Stronghold to Merkin Vineyards, with restaurants in between. Park once and stay on foot. Our Old Town Cottonwood wine tasting guide walks the street block by block.

4. Clarkdale (a few minutes past Cottonwood). Small, quiet and easy to overlook, which is part of the charm. Two worth knowing:

  • Southwest Wine Center at Yavapai College, 601 W Black Hills Drive, pours student-made wines from the college’s teaching winery. Visit Arizona lists the tasting room as open Thursday through Sunday, noon to 6 p.m.
  • Chateau Tumbleweed, 1151 W State Route 89A, began in 2011 with a three-barrel batch made by two couples, according to Visit Arizona, which lists hours of Thursday through Monday, noon to 7 p.m. It is the sister winery of DA Ranch.

5. Jerome (about 30 miles, 45 minutes to an hour). The old copper town clinging to Cleopatra Hill, with tasting rooms on and around Main Street and some of the best views in the valley. Caduceus Cellars pours at 158 Main Street, open 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday through Thursday and until 7 p.m. Friday and Saturday, per Caduceus. Passion Cellars, Cabal Cellars, Original Jerome Winery and others round out the hill. Our Jerome day trip guide covers the mine museum, the state park and lunch.

Off to the southeast: Camp Verde estates (about 35 minutes). Alcantara Vineyard, at 3445 S Grapevine Way on the Verde River, is the big one, with more than 20,000 vines and 17 varieties, according to Visit Arizona, which lists daily hours of 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. Alcantara’s hours have shifted seasonally in the past, so check the current hours before you build a day around it. A few smaller Camp Verde wineries open only Friday through Sunday.

Three Verde Valley Wine Trail routes that work

The single most common mistake we see is trying to hit every cluster in one day. Five tasting rooms across four towns sounds efficient at breakfast and feels like a lot of driving by 3 p.m. These routes keep the windshield time short and the order logical.

The half-day: Page Springs only (3 to 4 hours)

Leave Sedona around 11 a.m. and taste at two or three Page Springs and Cornville wineries, with a snack or light lunch at one of them. You are back in town by 3 or 4 p.m. with time for sunset. This is the route we recommend for first-timers and anyone with only an afternoon.

The classic full day: Page Springs to Old Town Cottonwood (5 to 6 hours)

Start at the vineyards in late morning while the patios are pleasant, taste at two estates, then drive about 15 to 20 minutes to Old Town Cottonwood. Have a late lunch on Main Street and finish with one or two tasting rooms on foot. Doing it in this order means the vineyard stops, which close earlier, come first, and the walkable town, where many rooms stay open into the evening on weekends, comes last.

The northwest loop: Cottonwood, Clarkdale and Jerome (6 hours)

Drive straight to Jerome first, while you are fresh for the switchbacks. Taste one or two rooms, eat lunch, walk the town, then come down the hill to Clarkdale and finish in Old Town Cottonwood. Coming downhill after tasting is not a substitute for a sober driver, but it does mean the steepest driving of the day happens before the first pour. Check Clarkdale’s days first, since Southwest Wine Center and Chateau Tumbleweed are not open every day of the week.

Two days: split the triangle

Day one, Page Springs and Camp Verde. Day two, Jerome, Clarkdale and Cottonwood. Staying a night in Cottonwood puts you minutes from Main Street and lets you walk to dinner. Our where to stay in Sedona guide compares the bases.

Who drives? The question the map cannot answer

Every route above assumes someone has a clear head behind the wheel. The trail’s own site says it plainly: touring includes visits to several wineries, and everyone is encouraged to designate a driver and drink responsibly. A flight is often four or five pours, and three stops adds up fast.

When to go, what to book, what to bring

When to go. Late October through November is one of the best windows of the year: harvest is wrapping up, the sycamores and cottonwoods along Oak Creek turn gold, and patio afternoons are still comfortable. Wednesday through Friday is quieter than the weekend. Mondays and Tuesdays have the most closures, so check every stop on your route before a weekday trip. Start by late morning; many vineyard tasting rooms close around 5 or 6 p.m.

What to book. A Tock reservation at Page Springs Cellars on weekends, a table for lunch in Cottonwood or Jerome on Saturdays, and your ride. Parties larger than six should call wineries ahead; Page Springs Cellars asks for advance notice for groups over six.

What to bring. Photo ID for everyone tasting, water, a light layer for creekside patios once the sun drops, and a cooler bag so bottles do not cook in the car. Comfortable shoes for Jerome’s steep streets. Download the Trail Passport if you like collecting check-ins.

Before you leave. Hours on this trail change with the seasons and some rooms close on short notice. Treat every time in this guide as a starting point and check the current hours with each winery the day before.

The bottom line

Use the Verde Valley Wine Trail map for the big picture, then plan by cluster: Page Springs for vineyards, Cottonwood for walking, Jerome for the views, and never more than two or three towns in a day.
