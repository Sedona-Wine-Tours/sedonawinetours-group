Restaurants in Cottonwood AZ: Where to Eat on an Old Town Wine Day

Restaurants in Cottonwood AZ for a wine day: breakfast on Main Street, Neapolitan pizza, a hilltop winery trattoria and a steakhouse dinner, with tips.

By Jim Reich · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

The best restaurants in Cottonwood AZ sit almost entirely on a few walkable blocks of North Main Street in Old Town, which makes it easy to pair a meal with wine tasting: Crema for breakfast or a courtyard lunch, Pizzeria Bocce for Neapolitan pizza, Nic’s for a steak-and-crab dinner, and Merkin Vineyards Hilltop Winery & Trattoria for a farm-to-table meal with a view just up the hill. The one thing to plan around is timing, because several of these kitchens keep short or seasonal hours.

We have been driving guests through the Verde Valley since 2004, and Old Town Cottonwood is where a lot of wine days either start or wind down. It is about 19 miles and 30 minutes from Sedona, and once you park you can walk to roughly a dozen tasting rooms and most of the restaurants below (we cover the tasting rooms themselves in our Old Town Cottonwood wine tasting guide). This post is the food half of that day. Hours are what each business or the local chamber publishes as of mid-November 2026, and some sources disagree with each other, so check the current hours before you go.

Restaurants in Cottonwood AZ for breakfast and lunch

Tasting on an empty stomach is the most common mistake we see, so start here.

Crema Craft Kitchen & Bar (917 N Main Street) is the courtyard cafe most people find first. It does espresso, breakfast, lunch and house gelato, and it pours local beer and wine. The Cottonwood Chamber of Commerce lists it as open every day from 7 AM to 4 PM (Cottonwood Chamber), while Sedona Monthly lists earlier closing times (Sedona Monthly). Our advice: treat Crema as a breakfast and early-lunch stop, not a mid-afternoon plan. Call (928) 649-5785 if you are cutting it close.

Red Rooster Cafe (901 N Main Street) is a few doors down and is the classic small-town breakfast spot on the strip. Its breakfast burrito has a following. We wrote more about it in our best breakfast in Sedona roundup, since plenty of Sedona visitors make the drive for it.

Queen B Vinyl Cafe is the oddball of the bunch in the best way: a record shop, barbershop and cafe in one, serving breakfast sandwiches and burgers, according to Sedona Monthly’s Cottonwood listings. It is a good fit for a group where half the people want to browse and half want to eat.

A practical tip for lunch: most Old Town tasting rooms open late morning, so a 10:30 or 11 AM breakfast-lunch at one of these spots lines up neatly with the first pour.

Merkin Vineyards Hilltop Winery & Trattoria: lunch or dinner with a view

If you only have one sit-down meal in Cottonwood, this is the one we suggest most often. Merkin Vineyards Hilltop Winery & Trattoria sits at 770 N Verde Heights Drive, a short drive up from Main Street, and it is a working winery with a farm-to-table kitchen and a greenhouse. Arizona Highways describes a regularly changing menu of pizzas and pastas, house-made duck egg pasta, and a patio one writer called the best in the Verde Valley (Arizona Highways). Sedona Monthly points to its signature “pink” pasta.

A few things to know:

  • It is open daily. Sedona Monthly lists 11 AM to 9 PM, and until 10 PM on Fridays and Saturdays. Confirm before a late dinner.
  • Reservations are recommended for parties of eight or more, per Arizona Highways. Smaller groups can usually walk in, though fall and spring weekends fill up.
  • The Ventura Room is reservation-only. It is a multi-course meal paired with Caduceus Cellars wines and includes a winery tour, which makes it a strong pick for an anniversary or a small celebration.
  • You can buy bottles to take home, which is handy if you find something at lunch you want for dinner back in Sedona.

Note that older guides still mention a Merkin “Osteria” on Main Street. Arizona Highways reports that the Hilltop location replaced it, so head up the hill rather than looking for it downtown.

Pizza, pub food and casual dinners in Old Town Cottonwood

Pizzeria Bocce (1060 N Main Street) is the casual dinner we hear the most praise about. Chef-owner Michelle Jurisin is certified by the Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana, and the menu centers on Neapolitan pies like the Margherita, plus salads, panini and a banana-and-Nutella dessert pizza (Arizona Highways). There is a marble bar, a wood-fired oven and an outdoor patio, and the chamber listing shows wine on tap (Cottonwood Chamber).

Bocce is mostly an evening place. The chamber lists the kitchen at 4 to 10 PM Monday through Saturday and a short Sunday window, while Sedona Monthly adds a Saturday noon opening. Either way, do not plan on it for a weekday lunch. Call (928) 202-3597 to check.

The Tavern Grille fills the old Rialto Theatre building on Main Street and is the dependable crowd-pleaser: burgers, sandwiches, salads and a big room that handles groups. Sedona Monthly notes a daily 3 to 5 PM happy hour (Sedona Monthly), which fits the gap between your last tasting and dinner.

Eden is a contemporary spot with burgers and salads that Sedona Monthly lists as open Tuesday through Saturday, so it is one to keep in mind for the middle of the week.

Where to eat in Cottonwood AZ for a special dinner

Nic’s Italian Steak & Crab House (925 N Main Street) is Old Town’s dress-up-a-little dinner, with steaks, crab, Italian dishes, a martini list, a wine list and nightly specials. It is dinner only: the chamber lists 5 to 9 PM Sunday through Thursday and 5 to 10 PM Friday and Saturday (Cottonwood Chamber). Call (928) 634-9626 for a table on a weekend, and plan on it filling up during holiday weeks.

Between Nic’s, Merkin’s Ventura Room and a late table at Bocce, Cottonwood covers the special-occasion dinner well enough that some couples skip the drive back to Sedona for dinner entirely and spend the whole afternoon and evening in Old Town. If that is your plan, settle who is driving before the first tasting, or let someone else drive.

A sample Old Town Cottonwood food and wine day

Here is how we would string it together for a relaxed Saturday:

  1. 10:30 AM: Breakfast or brunch at Crema or Red Rooster.
  2. 11:30 AM to 3 PM: Three or four tasting rooms on foot. Pace yourself with water between stops, and share flights if you want to try more places.
  3. 3 to 5 PM: A break, a walk through the shops, or a happy-hour snack at The Tavern Grille.
  4. 5:30 PM: Dinner at Pizzeria Bocce for casual, Nic’s for steak, or up the hill at Merkin for the patio and the sunset.

For a weekday, swap Bocce for Merkin or Tavern Grille at lunch, since Bocce’s kitchen does not open until late afternoon.

What to book, what to bring and when to go

What to book: Reserve dinner at Nic’s on weekends, and book Merkin ahead for groups of eight or more or for the Ventura Room. If you are visiting as a bigger group for a birthday or a company outing, our group and private event page covers how we handle larger parties, and our guide to Sedona wineries and vineyards helps you decide which tasting rooms belong in the plan.

What to bring: Comfortable shoes for Main Street, a light jacket for evenings on a patio (November nights drop quickly once the sun is down), and a cooler bag if you plan to buy bottles. If your dog is coming along, ask each restaurant about its current patio policy before you go, since policies change and we could not confirm them all.

When to go: Weekday lunches are the easiest time to walk in anywhere. Friday and Saturday evenings are busiest, and Sunday is the day to double-check, because several kitchens close early or keep short hours. Visit Arizona’s Cottonwood page is a good starting point for events that bring crowds to Old Town (Visit Arizona).

Getting there: Old Town Cottonwood is about 30 minutes from Sedona on 89A, and Main Street is flat and easy to walk once you park. If you would rather not drive at all, Sedona Wine Adventures runs wine tours from Sedona that serve Cottonwood; reach them at (928) 366-8476 or sedonawineadventures.com, or see the details on our Sedona Wine Adventures page. Ask when you book how lunch fits into the tour you choose.

Old Town’s restaurants are half the reason Cottonwood makes such an easy day in Sedona wine country, and if you want help matching the day to a tour, our Tour Chooser will point you to the right one.
