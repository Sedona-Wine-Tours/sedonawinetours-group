Sedona Without a Car: Free Shuttles, Buses and Walkable Areas

Visiting Sedona without a car? A local guide since 2004 explains the free trailhead shuttle, Shuttle Connect, the Verde Shuttle bus and where you can walk.

By Jim Reich · November 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Yes, you can do Sedona without a car, and more visitors pull it off every year. Between the city’s free trailhead shuttle, the on-demand Sedona Shuttle Connect, the Verde Shuttle bus along State Routes 89A and 179, and a few genuinely walkable pockets of town, you can see a lot of red rock country while your keys stay at home. The trick is knowing which days each service runs and planning your bigger outings around them.

We have been driving guests around Sedona since 2004, and a big share of the people we pick up arrived without a rental car. Here is how getting around Sedona without a car actually works right now, with the schedules and fares we could verify and links to the official sources so you can check the latest.

How big is Sedona, really?

Sedona is smaller than people expect. Visit Sedona describes the city limits as roughly 6 miles long by 3 miles wide. It sorts into a few areas that matter for car-free planning:

  • Uptown Sedona, the walkable tourist core along 89A with shops, restaurants, tasting rooms and views of the canyon mouth.
  • Gallery Row and Tlaquepaque along State Route 179, just south of Uptown. Visit Sedona puts Gallery Row about a 15-minute stroll from Uptown.
  • West Sedona, the long commercial stretch of 89A where locals shop and eat. Visit Sedona notes most of its attractions sit within about a 2-mile stretch of the highway.
  • The Village of Oak Creek, about 7 miles south on 179, near Bell Rock and Courthouse Butte.

If you stay in Uptown or along the 179 gallery corridor, you can walk to dinner, shopping and art most days. Stay in West Sedona or the Village and you will lean on the shuttles and buses below a little more.

The free Sedona Shuttle trailhead routes

The City of Sedona runs the Sedona Shuttle, and its Trailhead Routes are the single best tool for hiking Sedona without a car. They are free and they solve the biggest headache in town: trailhead parking.

According to Visit Sedona, the Trailhead Shuttles run Thursday through Sunday, year-round, and the ground transportation page lists hours of 7 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. on those days. Buses leave from three park-and-ride lots, which the City of Sedona names as Posse Grounds Park and the West SR-89A and North SR-179 park-and-ride lots. The city lists the trailheads served as Cathedral Rock, Little Horse, Soldiers Pass, Dry Creek Vista and Mescal.

A few practical notes from our guests’ experience:

  • Go early. The first buses of the morning are the most pleasant hiking hours from October through April, and you beat the late-morning crowds at Cathedral Rock.
  • Watch the last bus. If you are deep into a longer hike, know when the final return runs before you set out.
  • Monday through Wednesday is different. Outside peak periods, plan your trailhead days for Thursday through Sunday, or choose hikes you can reach from where you are staying.

The city sometimes expands service. For spring break 2026, the city ran the shuttle seven days a week from Feb. 26 through April 26. Over last year’s holidays, the city ran daily service from Dec. 18, 2025 through Jan. 4, 2026, except Christmas Day. We have not seen a 2026 holiday schedule announced yet, so check the city’s news page before you lock in December plans.

For non-hiking ideas you can reach on foot or by shuttle, our guide to things to do in Sedona without hiking has a dozen picks.

Sedona Shuttle Connect: the on-demand ride

The newer half of the city system is Sedona Shuttle Connect, an on-demand, curb-to-curb ride within a designated service area. Think of it as a small-town version of hailing a ride, run by the city.

Per Visit Sedona, Connect costs $2 per ride and runs Thursdays through Sundays from 6:30 a.m. to 8:30 p.m., with marked pickup and drop-off signs at Hillside Sedona and Uptown Sedona. The City of Sedona’s December 2025 release describes the service area as West Sedona, Uptown and the Chapel of the Holy Cross/Mystic Hills area, with seniors 60 and over riding for $1. Rides to the Chapel drop off and pick up at the Mystic Trailhead. You book through the TransLoc app or by calling (928) 203-5152.

Connect is ideal for the in-between trips: West Sedona hotel to Uptown for dinner, or a morning at the Chapel of the Holy Cross without fighting for one of its few parking spaces.

The Verde Shuttle: Sedona’s everyday bus

The Verde Shuttle, run by Cottonwood Area Transit, is the workhorse bus between Cottonwood and Uptown Sedona, with stops along 89A and 179 in town. It is the car-free answer on the days the city shuttle does not run, and it connects Sedona to Old Town Cottonwood’s tasting rooms and restaurants.

The City of Cottonwood’s fare page lists these fares:

  • One-way: $2 regular, $1 discounted
  • Trips within Sedona: $1 regular, 50 cents discounted
  • All Access day pass: $6 regular, $3 discounted

Discounted fares apply to seniors 60 and over, veterans, students with ID and riders with disabilities. You can pay in cash or buy passes through the Token Transit app, or in person at City of Sedona Finance, 102 Roadrunner Drive.

The Verde Shuttle rider guide describes hourly service seven days a week, with the first bus leaving Cottonwood at 6 a.m. and the last bus leaving Uptown Sedona at 10 p.m. That guide dates from 2022, so check the current times before you count on a late bus home. Sedona stops include Tlaquepaque, the Uptown municipal parking lot, Hillside, the Whole Foods area on 89A, Dry Creek Road and the Yavapai College campus in West Sedona. Each bus has a wheelchair lift and a bike rack for up to three bikes.

Getting to Sedona without a car

The trip into town is the hardest part of going car-free. Sedona has no commercial airline service, and most visitors fly into Phoenix Sky Harbor, about two hours south. Visit Sedona’s ground transportation page notes year-round airport shuttle service with multiple daily trips between Phoenix and Sedona, along with private car services. Book ahead for holiday weekends, when seats fill.

Rideshare apps work in town, but in our experience availability can be thin early in the morning, late at night and out at the trailheads, so do not plan a sunrise hike around catching one. If you are weighing the drive instead, our Phoenix to Sedona drive guide covers the routes and stops.

Wine country without a car

Here is the one outing where skipping the rental car is a gift rather than a compromise. The Verde Valley wine trail is spread across Page Springs, Cornville, Old Town Cottonwood, Jerome and Camp Verde, and none of the vineyard stops on Page Springs Road are on a bus line. Even Old Town Cottonwood, which you can reach on the Verde Shuttle, makes for a long, late ride home after a few flights.

What to book, what to bring and when to go

What to book: Lodging in Uptown or along the 179 gallery corridor if you want to walk most places. Your airport transfer, well ahead of holiday weekends. Any wine day or guided outing, so pickup is set before you arrive.

What to bring: A phone with the TransLoc and Token Transit apps installed, a little cash for the Verde Shuttle, a daypack with water and layers for trailhead days, and comfortable walking shoes. Sedona sidewalks come and go, so expect some walking along road shoulders.

When to go: Build your hiking days around Thursday through Sunday when the free trailhead shuttle runs, and save Monday through Wednesday for Uptown, galleries, spas and wine country. Fall and spring are the easiest seasons for walking between stops, and winter afternoons are mild enough for it most days.
