Best Time for Wine Tasting in Sedona: A Guide's Season-by-Season Pick

The best time for wine tasting in Sedona, from a guide since 2004: harvest, festival dates, weather by month, quiet weekdays and what each season offers.

By Jim Reich · November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

The best time for wine tasting in Sedona is mid-October through mid-November, when harvest is in the cellar, afternoons sit in the 60s and 70s, and the sycamores along Oak Creek turn gold. A close second is March through early May, with mild weather, green vineyards and two of the valley’s best wine festivals. But the honest answer is that Verde Valley tasting rooms pour all year, and every season has a reason to come.

We have been guiding guests through Sedona wine country since 2004, and “when should we come?” is one of the first questions we get. Here is how the year actually plays out, month by month, from the people who drive these roads every day.

Why timing matters in Verde Valley wine country

The Verde Valley is a high-desert growing region, recognized as its own American Viticultural Area in 2021. According to the Verde Valley AVA, the valley averages under 14 inches of rain a year, and the swing between daytime highs and overnight lows typically runs 30 to 40 degrees. Hot days build sugar in the grapes; cool nights hold on to acidity and aroma. More than 40 varieties are planted, from Malvasia Bianca and Viognier to Syrah, Petite Sirah, Tannat and Barbera.

That same climate shapes your visit. Sedona sits around 4,350 feet, and the wineries of Page Springs, Cornville and Camp Verde sit lower and run a little warmer. Here are Sedona’s average highs and lows, from NOAA’s 1991–2020 climate normals as compiled by Current Results:

  • January: 58 / 34
  • March: 68 / 40
  • April: 75 / 44
  • May: 84 / 53
  • July: 97 / 68
  • September: 89 / 61
  • October: 79 / 51
  • November: 67 / 41
  • December: 58 / 33

In practice, that means a winery patio is a delight from about 11 a.m. to 4 p.m. most of the year, a sun-trap in July, and a sweater-and-heater spot on a December afternoon.

Fall (September to November): harvest, color and our top pick

Fall is the best time for wine tasting in Sedona, and the reason is simple: everything lines up at once.

Harvest and crush. Verde Valley growers usually pick in August and September, and the crush can stretch into October depending on the year. A 2025 report in Flagstaff Business News described growers harvesting through August and September, with friends, family and students out picking at first light. Prescott Woman Magazine notes that the monsoon drives the timing: hail can damage fruit, heavy rain can delay picking, and a dry year compresses the whole season. If you want to see bins of grapes and smell fermenting must, come in September. If you want the cellar work done and winemakers with time to talk, come in late October or November.

Weather. September can still be hot (an average high near 89), and afternoon storms linger into early fall some years. October, with highs near 79, is about as perfect as Arizona gets. November cools into the 60s, with clear, crisp afternoons.

Color. The cottonwoods and sycamores along Oak Creek and the Verde River usually turn from late October into mid-November, which puts Page Springs at its prettiest. Our guide to Sedona in November covers what else is happening this month.

Events. Old Town Cottonwood’s biggest street festival, Walkin’ on Main, is set for Saturday, November 14, 2026, according to the Verde Valley Wine Consortium, with wine tasting tickets sold online. Sedona WineFest has traditionally been held in late September at Posse Grounds Park; check the organizers for the next date before you plan around it.

The catch. Fall is also Sedona’s busiest season. Weekends fill fast, hotel rates climb, and tasting room patios get crowded by early afternoon on Saturdays.

Spring (March to May): green vines and festival season

Spring is our second favorite season for wine tasting, and some years it edges out fall.

What is happening in the vineyard. Vines wake up in spring. Bud break and the first green leaves turn the rows from bare wood to bright green, and the creekside wineries feel lush against the red rock. New releases start showing up in tasting rooms.

Weather. March averages about 68 for a high, April about 75, and May about 84. March can still bring a cold snap or a windy day, so pack a layer. Late April and early May are about as reliable as it gets.

Festivals. Two of the region’s best wine events fall in spring, both hosted by the Verde Valley Wine Consortium:

  • Camp Verde Pecan and Wine Festival. The 2026 edition ran March 21 and 22 at Camp Verde Community Field, with free admission, about 15 Arizona wineries, and early-bird tasting packages of $20 for a glass and six tasting tickets ($25 at the door), per the consortium. Only service animals were allowed.
  • Verde Valley Wine Festival. The 2026 festival was Saturday, May 9, at Riverfront Park in Cottonwood, with new releases from 20 local wineries, food trucks and live music, per Visit Sedona. It is usually tied to Mother’s Day weekend.

Check the 2027 dates before you book; both events have been held at roughly the same time each year.

The catch. Spring break weeks in March and the Easter and Memorial Day weekends bring Sedona’s other big crowds. Midweek is noticeably calmer.

Summer (June to August): early starts and cool tasting rooms

Summer is the least popular season for wine tasting here, and that can work in your favor.

Weather. June and July average in the mid to upper 90s in Sedona, and the lower wineries run warmer still. Monsoon season brings humidity, dramatic afternoon storms and the occasional flash flood warning from roughly mid-June through September.

How to make it work. Start early and finish early. We like an 11 a.m. first pour, with indoor tasting rooms or shaded patios in the hottest part of the day, and Old Town Cottonwood’s walkable Main Street, where you duck out of the sun between rooms. Bring more water than you think you need, and keep bottles out of a hot car.

The upside. Fewer people, easier reservations, monsoon light shows over the red rocks in the evening, and, by August, the first picks of the year in the vineyards.

Winter (December to February): quiet rooms and the best conversations

Winter is our underrated season. Days are short and mornings are cold, but afternoons often reach the high 50s under clear skies, and the tasting rooms are the quietest they will be all year.

Why we like it. Winemakers and staff have more time to talk. Reds taste right on a cold day. And many wineries stay open year-round; Page Springs Cellars, for example, says on its FAQ page that it is open 365 days a year. Smaller rooms may shorten their winter hours, so check the current hours before you go. Our list of wineries open on Sundays and Mondays helps with the early-week gaps.

What changes. The vines are dormant and bare, so the vineyard views are less dramatic. A storm can bring snow to Flagstaff and a dusting to the red rocks, and roads up the Mogollon Rim can close. The holidays (Christmas week through New Year’s) and Presidents’ Day weekend are busy; the weeks in between are wonderfully calm.

Best days of the week and times of day

Season matters, but the day of the week matters almost as much.

  • Best days: Wednesday through Friday. Rooms are open, staff are fresh, and the Saturday crowds have not arrived.
  • Busiest days: Saturday afternoons, especially in October, November, March and April.
  • Watch out for: Monday and Tuesday, when some smaller wineries close or shorten hours.
  • Best time of day: Start between 10:30 and 11:30 a.m. Most vineyard tasting rooms close around 5 or 6 p.m., so a late start leaves you rushing.
  • Light: Late afternoon in fall and spring gives you the best red-rock glow on the drive back to Sedona.

When to go, what to book, what to bring

When to go. For the full experience, mid-October through mid-November, Wednesday through Friday. For festivals and green vineyards, late March through early May. For quiet rooms and lower rates, January and February.

What to book.

  • Hotel rooms early for fall and spring weekends.
  • Tasting reservations wherever the winery takes them, especially on Saturdays.
  • A driver. Mountain roads and wine do not mix, and Page Springs Road is two lanes with curves as it drops to the creek. What to bring.

  • Photo ID for everyone tasting.
  • Layers. A 30-degree swing between morning and afternoon is normal.
  • Sunscreen and a hat, even in winter.
  • Water, and a cooler bag for bottles.
  • Comfortable shoes for gravel paths and Main Street sidewalks.

The bottom line

If you can pick any time, come mid-October through mid-November on a weekday; if not, spring is a close second, and even summer and winter reward a little planning.
