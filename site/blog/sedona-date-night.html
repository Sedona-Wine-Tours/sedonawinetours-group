Sedona Date Night: 10 Romantic Ideas From a Local Guide Since 2004

Sedona date night ideas from a guide since 2004: sunset at Airport Mesa, red rock view dinners, wine bars, Tlaquepaque at night, stargazing and what to book.

By Jim Reich · November 4, 2026 · 7 min read

The best Sedona date night follows a simple order: watch the sunset from a red rock overlook, sit down to dinner with a view while the cliffs are still glowing, and finish under one of the darkest skies of any town in the country. Add a glass of Arizona wine somewhere along the way and you have an evening most couples remember long after the trip.

We have been showing couples around Sedona and the Verde Valley since 2004, and we hear the same question every week: “Where should we go for a special night out?” Here are the date ideas we give our own friends, plus the timing and booking details that make them work.

Plan your Sedona date night around the sunset

In Sedona, the sunset is the main event, and everything else is scheduled around it. In November and through the winter, the sun drops behind the western rocks in the late afternoon, around 5:15 to 5:35 p.m., so a classic date night starts earlier than you might expect. In late spring and summer it shifts past 7 p.m.

Our rule of thumb:

  • Arrive at your sunset spot 45 to 60 minutes early. The light on the rocks is often best in the half hour before the sun actually sets.
  • Book dinner for 30 to 45 minutes after sunset, or book a table with a view for just before sunset if you want to watch it from your seat.
  • Bring a jacket. Even on a warm fall day, temperatures fall fast once the sun is gone.

1. Sunset at Airport Mesa

The Sedona Airport Scenic Overlook, partway up Airport Road, is the easiest big-view sunset in town. You look across West Sedona toward the red cliffs, and on a clear evening the whole valley turns orange and then pink. According to the Sedona Airport, parking at the overlook costs $3, the receipt works as an all-day pass, and you should arrive an hour before the published sunset to beat the traffic. Overnight parking and camping are not allowed.

If the lot is full, park lower and walk, or pick a quieter spot. Visit Sedona’s couples guide also suggests Sugarloaf and Vultee Arch for sunsets; both take a little more effort, so bring headlamps for the walk out.

2. Dinner with a red rock view

Sedona has more view restaurants than any town its size has a right to, and a window table at sunset is the heart of a date night here. Two we send couples to again and again:

  • Mariposa Latin Inspired Grill. It sits on a bluff at 700 State Route 89A, with South American-inspired dishes cooked over a wood-fired grill and in a wood-burning oven. Visit Sedona lists dinner from 5 p.m. daily, and the restaurant promises red rock views from every table. Sunset reservations go first, so book early.
  • Mesa Grill at the Sedona Airport. A relaxed, less formal choice up on Airport Mesa, close to the overlook, which makes it an easy pairing with a sunset stop. Check the current hours before you go.

For a full list, see our guide to Sedona restaurants with a view, where we compare the best tables for sunset by price and mood.

3. Romantic restaurants in Sedona without a big view

Sometimes the best date night is about candlelight and good wine rather than scenery. Dahl & Di Luca in West Sedona is the local favorite for an Italian dinner, with homemade pasta and a long wine list. At Tlaquepaque, René offers a refined, European-style courtyard setting that feels made for anniversaries. Both are popular on weekends, so book ahead.

4. A glass of Arizona wine before or after dinner

You do not need to leave town to taste Arizona wine. Vino Di Sedona at 2575 W State Route 89A calls itself Sedona’s fine wine and craft beer bar and wine store, with more than 900 wines (many from Arizona), charcuterie and flatbreads, daily happy hour and live music, according to Visit Sedona. It is a relaxed place for a pre-dinner glass, or for a nightcap after an early sunset dinner. Uptown and West Sedona also have several winery tasting rooms; our guide to wine tasting in Sedona explains where to taste and what it costs.

5. Make it a wine country day date

Some of our favorite date nights actually start at 10 a.m. Spend the day tasting in the Verde Valley, come back to your hotel for a rest, and head out for sunset and dinner. Page Springs, Cornville and Old Town Cottonwood are all within about 30 minutes of Sedona, and fall and winter weekdays are wonderfully quiet in the tasting rooms.

The one catch is driving. Wine tasting and winding roads do not mix, and you both deserve to enjoy the day. That is where we come in:

6. Tlaquepaque after dark

Tlaquepaque Arts and Shopping Village is lovely by day and even better at night, when the courtyards, archways and fountains glow under the lights. Stroll the galleries before they close, have dinner on a patio, and walk the courtyards afterward.

The holidays are the most magical time to go. Tlaquepaque’s Christmas tree lighting is on Friday, December 4, 2026, from 5 to 6:30 p.m., according to Visit Sedona. The village’s Festival of Lights is a free evening tradition in December when 6,000 luminarias are lit at 5 p.m. as the bell tower tolls, with live music and free cider; check Tlaquepaque’s calendar for this year’s date. Both nights are crowded, so arrive early and make dinner reservations well in advance.

7. Stargazing under a Dark Sky Community

Sedona was named the world’s eighth International Dark Sky Community in 2014, and DarkSky International still lists it today. On a clear, moonless night you can see the Milky Way from just outside town. Visit Sedona suggests Beaverhead Flat Scenic Overlook, the Aerie Trailhead, Fay Canyon and Merry-Go-Round Rock as good dark spots.

For a date, keep it simple: a blanket, a thermos of something warm, red-light flashlights and a stargazing app. Check the moon phase before you go; a new moon gives the darkest sky. Our Sedona stargazing guide covers more spots and the best months.

8. A film, opera or show at the Mary D. Fisher Theatre

The Sedona International Film Festival’s Mary D. Fisher Theatre hosts films, concerts, comedy and Met Opera screenings year-round, not just during the February festival. This month’s calendar includes a “Carmen” Met Opera encore on November 21. Pair an early dinner in West Sedona with a show for a date night that does not depend on the weather.

9. A couples spa afternoon

Sedona’s resort spas are made for couples, and a side-by-side massage followed by a fireside drink is a classic date. Weekday afternoons in the off-season, especially January and February, are the easiest to book. Ask about red rock view treatment rooms and whether the spa includes pool or hot tub time.

10. A sunrise date instead

For couples who are not night owls, flip the script. Watch sunrise from a quiet overlook, then linger over breakfast. Our best breakfast in Sedona post has our favorite spots. Mornings are calm, the light on the rocks is soft, and you have the rest of the day free for a wine country afternoon.

What to book, what to bring, when to go

When to go. Fall and spring offer the most comfortable evenings for patio dinners and sunsets. Winter brings early sunsets, long dark nights for stargazing and holiday lights at Tlaquepaque. Summer evenings are warm, but July and August monsoon clouds can hide the stars.

What to book.

  • Dinner reservations, especially sunset tables on Fridays and Saturdays and on holiday weekends.
  • Spa appointments, at least a week ahead in busy seasons.
  • A driver for any wine country day. For a special occasion with a larger group, such as an anniversary trip with friends, see our groups and celebrations page.

What to bring.

  • Warm layers for after sunset, even in spring and fall.
  • Headlamps or red-light flashlights for overlooks and stargazing.
  • Comfortable shoes for Tlaquepaque’s cobblestones.
  • Photo ID for wine bars and tasting rooms.
  • A few dollars in cash or a card for parking at the Airport Scenic Overlook.
