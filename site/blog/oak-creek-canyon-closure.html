Oak Creek Canyon Closure: What's Open in Sedona After the 2026 Flood

SR 89A is open from Sedona to Slide Rock but closed north to Flagstaff after the Nov. 1 flood. What's open, what's closed and how to plan a Sedona trip now.

By Jim Reich · November 9, 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer on the Oak Creek Canyon closure: as of November 9, 2026, State Route 89A is open from Sedona up to Slide Rock State Park, but the switchbacks north of Slide Rock toward Flagstaff remain closed with no reopening date, so the drive between Sedona and Flagstaff runs through SR 179 and Interstate 17 instead. Much of Sedona and the Verde Valley is open and welcoming visitors, but creekside areas are still recovering, and a few specific places need a phone call before you go.

We have guided in red rock country since 2004, and the November 1 flood is one of the hardest weeks this community has had. Our hearts are with the canyon residents, business owners and Verde Valley neighbors who lost homes, inventory and income. This post is our plain-language rundown of what we know today, with links to the official sources that will have newer information than we do.

What happened on November 1

Heavy rain on Sunday, November 1 sent Oak Creek well above flood stage. According to the ADOT release carried by Signals AZ, the rising creek undermined portions of the highway through the canyon and exposed guardrail supports, and crews found boulders, mud and fallen trees on the roadway. Dozens of people were rescued from Oak Creek, and some canyon residents and visitors were stranded for days.

The Mayor of Sedona signed a local Proclamation of Emergency on November 2, Signals AZ reported, and the city has posted recovery information at sedonaaz.gov/floodrecovery. Downstream, Yavapai County reported 253 homes evacuated and 466 homes impacted before it lifted all evacuation orders on November 4, according to the Arizona Daily Star. Camp Verde took some of the worst of it.

Is 89A open through Oak Creek Canyon?

Partly. Here is where the Oak Creek Canyon closure stands, based on ADOT’s update on Friday, November 6, as reported by KJZZ and Signals AZ:

  • Open: SR 89A from the Owenby Way roundabout in Uptown Sedona north to Slide Rock State Park.
  • Closed: SR 89A north of Slide Rock State Park to the Oak Creek Vista overlook at the top of the switchbacks. ADOT says to expect a long-term closure, and there is no estimated date to reopen the full route between Sedona and Flagstaff.
  • From Flagstaff: southbound traffic can go as far as the Oak Creek Vista overlook, but travel down into the canyon is not allowed during repairs.
  • No roadside parking: ADOT says parking along the open stretch of 89A is strictly prohibited, and large vehicles may have few places to turn around.

The stretch north of Slide Rock is also where many of the canyon’s lodges and cabins sit. Arizona’s Family reported that Coconino County is working on a plan for local and business traffic, but nothing had been announced as of this writing. If you have a reservation in the upper canyon, call the property directly.

Road status is changing week to week. Before any drive, check AZ511, the AZ511 app or dial 511.

How to get between Sedona and Flagstaff now

Until the canyon reopens, ADOT is routing Sedona-Flagstaff traffic over SR 179 south to Interstate 17, then north on I-17 to Flagstaff. It is all highway and it is the way most of our guests arrive from Phoenix anyway, so it is easy to follow. It takes longer than the canyon drive, so build in extra time, especially for an early morning Grand Canyon start.

If Flagstaff or the Grand Canyon is on your itinerary, our Sedona to Grand Canyon day trip guide covers the I-17 route along with the timing, and our Grand Canyon in winter post has the latest on what is open at the South Rim.

What’s open and what’s closed in Sedona right now

The Greater Sedona Chamber’s flood update said most businesses across the region were unaffected and welcoming guests. That matches what we see in town. Here is the picture by area, with the caveat that creekside sites are the ones still in flux.

Generally open:

  • Red rock viewpoints and trails away from the creek. Airport Mesa, the Chapel area, and the trailheads in West Sedona and along SR 179 are not in the canyon. Check the Coconino National Forest alerts page for any individual closure before you hike.
  • SR 179. The Chamber reported it reopened in both directions between Ranger Road and Schnebly Hill Road after a short flood closure.
  • Tlaquepaque. The Chamber said the village is welcoming visitors back, but some galleries, shops and restaurants were still working toward reopening. Check the hours of a specific business before you go.
  • Wine country. The tasting rooms in Page Springs, Cornville, Old Town Cottonwood and Jerome are southwest of Sedona, reached by 89A toward Cottonwood and Page Springs Road, not through Oak Creek Canyon. As always, check a room’s current hours before you drive.

Closed or restricted:

  • West Fork Trail. The Forest Service had already closed West Fork Trail No. 108 and its drainage through February 27, 2027 under its extended closure order, and the trailhead is also beyond the open stretch of 89A. Our West Fork Trail post lists alternatives.
  • Creekside day-use sites. As of the Chamber’s last update, access at Crescent Moon, Grasshopper Point and Slide Rock State Park was restricted or unconfirmed. The road now reaches Slide Rock, but check Arizona State Parks for the park’s own status before you go.
  • Oak Creek itself. The Chamber asked visitors to stay out of the creek because of flood hazards and reported sewage contamination. This is not the week for wading.
  • Center for the New Age on SR 179, which the Chamber said sustained significant flood damage and was temporarily closed.

Should you still come to Sedona this month?

For most trips, yes, with a little more planning than usual. Sedona’s economy runs on visitors, and the businesses that are open need you now more than ever. The red rocks, the restaurants and the Verde Valley wine rooms are why most people come, and they are reachable.

A few things are worth doing before you go:

  1. Call your lodging. Confirm your reservation and ask about access, especially if you are booked anywhere near the creek or in the upper canyon. Some early news reports said the city was limiting new lodging guests in the first days after the flood, so if you booked recently, confirm directly with your property and check the city’s flood recovery page.
  2. Check AZ511 the morning you drive. Closures and lane restrictions can change with repair work.
  3. Reshuffle canyon plans. Swap Slide Rock and West Fork for a creek-free day: a red rock viewpoint in the morning, Tlaquepaque or Uptown at midday, and a wine afternoon in Page Springs or Cottonwood. Our things to do in Sedona without hiking list is a good starting point.
  4. Be patient and kind. Many people working in hospitality here are cleaning up their own homes and neighborhoods while they show up for guests.

What to book, what to bring, when to go

Book: your lodging (and a confirmation call), dinner reservations for Thanksgiving week, and anything with a time slot. If wine tasting is part of the plan, a guided day takes the driving and detour math off your plate, since our routes run through Page Springs, Cornville, Cottonwood and Jerome, not the canyon. Our Sedona wineries and vineyards guide shows the rooms, and you can call Sedona Wine Tours at (928) 504-2445 or email tours@sedonawinetours.group to check dates.

Bring: layers for cold mornings and mild afternoons, a phone with the AZ511 app, and shoes you do not mind getting muddy on trails that are still drying out.

When to go: right now is a quieter-than-usual window before the Thanksgiving rush. If your trip depends on the upper canyon, Slide Rock or West Fork, watch the official pages above and consider pushing those pieces to spring.

We will keep watching ADOT and the Forest Service and update our posts as the canyon reopens. In the meantime, Sedona’s wine country is open and only a short, canyon-free drive away, and the Tour Chooser will help you find the right day for your group.
