Wineries Open Sunday in Sedona (and Monday): A Guide's Day-by-Day List

Which wineries are open Sunday near Sedona, and which still pour on Monday? A local guide's list for Page Springs, Cottonwood, Jerome and Camp Verde.

By Jim Reich · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Good news if your Sedona trip runs through the weekend: most of the wineries near Sedona are open on Sunday, including every major estate in Page Springs, the big vineyard in Camp Verde and nearly all of Old Town Cottonwood. Monday is where it gets tricky. A handful of favorites close Monday (and often Tuesday), but you can still build a full day of tasting if you know which doors are open. Here is the list we work from, after more than twenty years of running wine days seven days a week.

One rule before the details: hours in wine country shift with the seasons, private events and staffing. Everything below was checked against each winery’s own site or the official tourism listings this week, but call or check the current hours the day before you go.

Wineries open Sunday near Sedona

Sunday is one of the best tasting days of the week here. Almost everything is open, the tasting rooms are a touch calmer than Saturday by mid-afternoon, and the weekend-only boutique wineries are pouring.

Page Springs and Cornville (20 to 30 minutes from Sedona)

  • Page Springs Cellars, 1500 N. Page Springs Road, is open 365 days a year, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday through Wednesday and until 8 p.m. Thursday through Saturday, according to its FAQ page. Tastings book through Tock, and parties over six should give advance notice.
  • Oak Creek Vineyards & Winery, 1555 N. Page Springs Road, is listed by Visit Arizona as open daily, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.
  • Javelina Leap Vineyard, 1565 N. Page Springs Road, is listed as open every day, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m., per Visit Arizona.
  • D.A. Ranch, 1901 Dancing Apache Road, is generally open Wednesday through Sunday, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m., and may close for weddings or private events, per Visit Sedona. Reservations are needed only for groups of 13 or more.
  • Cove Mesa Vineyard in Cornville pours Sundays as well; check the current hours with the winery before you go.

Old Town Cottonwood (about 30 minutes)

  • Arizona Stronghold, 1023 N. Main Street, opens at noon every day and closes at 6 p.m. Sunday through Tuesday, per its tasting room page. Seating is first come, first served; groups of eight or more should call ahead.
  • Burning Tree Cellars, 1040 N. Main Street, is listed as open noon to 7 p.m. Sunday through Thursday, per Visit Arizona.
  • Merkin Vineyards Tasting Room & Osteria is a full restaurant with wine, and stays open into the evening, which makes it a reliable Sunday dinner stop.

Jerome, Clarkdale and Camp Verde

  • Caduceus Cellars in Jerome, 158 Main Street, pours 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday through Thursday, per Caduceus.
  • Chateau Tumbleweed and the Southwest Wine Center in Clarkdale are both open Sundays (Chateau Tumbleweed Thursday through Monday, the Wine Center Thursday through Sunday).
  • Alcantara Vineyard, 3445 S. Grapevine Way in Camp Verde, says it is open daily, 11 a.m. to 5 p.m., on its own site.
  • Salt Mine Wine and Clear Creek Vineyards in Camp Verde are weekend wineries, so Sunday is one of the few days to catch them.

Wineries open Monday near Sedona

Monday takes a little more planning, but it is far from a lost day. Here is what we can count on most Mondays:

  • Page Springs Cellars (open every day of the year)
  • Oak Creek Vineyards and Javelina Leap, both listed as open daily, right next door to Page Springs Cellars on the same road
  • Alcantara Vineyard in Camp Verde (listed daily, 11 a.m. to 5 p.m.)
  • Arizona Stronghold and Burning Tree Cellars in Old Town Cottonwood, plus several other Main Street rooms
  • Caduceus Cellars in Jerome
  • Chateau Tumbleweed in Clarkdale, which runs Thursday through Monday

That is enough for a classic Page Springs morning and an Old Town Cottonwood afternoon without missing a beat. In fact, the three neighbors along Page Springs Road are close enough that you can taste at all of them in one easy half-day on a Monday.

Who closes on Monday (and Tuesday)

These are the stops that most often trip up Monday plans:

  • D.A. Ranch closes Monday and Tuesday.
  • Southwest Wine Center in Clarkdale opens Thursday through Sunday only.
  • Salt Mine Wine and Clear Creek Vineyards in Camp Verde are weekend-only.
  • Chateau Tumbleweed is closed Tuesday and Wednesday, so it is a Monday option but not a Tuesday one.

Our Sedona wineries and vineyards guide lists every stop we visit along with the days each one is closed, and we keep it current as the schedules change.

Sample Sunday and Monday wine days

A Sunday “everything is open” day. Start at a Page Springs estate around 11 a.m., taste at a second vineyard on the same road, then drive about 15 to 20 minutes to Old Town Cottonwood for a late lunch and one or two tasting rooms on foot. If you want something rarer, swap the afternoon for Camp Verde and catch the weekend-only boutique wineries.

A Monday that still works. Page Springs Cellars at opening, then Javelina Leap or Oak Creek Vineyards next door, lunch in Cottonwood, and Arizona Stronghold before it closes at 6 p.m. Or head to Camp Verde for Alcantara, which wraps up at 5 p.m., so go there first.

In town on a Sunday night. Several Sedona tasting rooms and wine bars pour Arizona wine near your hotel, which is handy if you want a glass after a hike without driving to the valley. What to book, what to bring, when to go

When to go. Late October through November is prime tasting weather, with gold sycamores along Oak Creek and comfortable patio afternoons. Start by 11 a.m. or noon; many vineyard rooms close at 5 or 6 p.m., even on Sunday. Holiday weekends, including Thanksgiving weekend, fill up quickly, so plan ahead.

What to book. A Tock reservation at Page Springs Cellars on weekends, a lunch table in Cottonwood on Sunday, and a call ahead anywhere your group is larger than six to eight people. And a ride, so nobody has to choose between driving and tasting.

What to bring. Photo ID for everyone tasting, water, a light layer for creekside patios once the sun drops, and a cooler bag so bottles do not bake in the car.

Before you leave. Re-check every stop the day before. Private events and seasonal changes close rooms with little notice, especially on weekends.

The bottom line

On Sunday, nearly all of Sedona wine country is open; on Monday, stick with Page Springs Road, Old Town Cottonwood, Alcantara and Jerome, and skip D.A. Ranch and the Clarkdale and Camp Verde boutiques.
