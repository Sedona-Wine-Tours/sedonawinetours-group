Thanksgiving in Sedona 2026: Dinners, What's Open and a Weekend Plan

Thanksgiving in Sedona 2026 falls on Nov. 26. A local guide since 2004 covers holiday dinners, what's open and closed, weather and a four-day weekend plan.

By Jim Reich · November 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Thanksgiving in Sedona lands on Thursday, November 26 this year, and it is one of the best weeks of the year to be here: cool, clear days, the last gold cottonwoods along Oak Creek, and resort dinners with red rock views. The catch is that the whole town fills up from Wednesday through Sunday, so the people who have the best holiday are the ones who book dinner, lodging and anything with a time slot now.

We have spent every Thanksgiving week in Sedona since 2004, and the same questions come up every year: where to eat, what is open on the day itself, what the weather does, and how to fill the long weekend. Here is our guide, with the 2026 details we could verify and links to the official pages so you can confirm before you go.

Thanksgiving in Sedona: what the week feels like

Sedona sits at roughly 4,350 feet, so late November is real autumn. Based on NOAA’s 1991-2020 normals, Sedona averages a high of 67°F and a low of 41°F in November, and about six nights in the month dip to freezing or below. In practice that means T-shirt-or-light-jacket afternoons in the sun and genuinely cold mornings and evenings. A puffy jacket for sunset dinner on a patio is not overkill.

Crowds are the other half of the story. From the Wednesday before Thanksgiving through Sunday, Sedona is as busy as a spring break weekend. State Route 179 backs up between the Village of Oak Creek and Uptown in the late morning, popular trailhead lots fill early, and walk-in restaurant tables are hard to find after 5 p.m. None of that should scare you off. It just rewards an early start, a dinner reservation and a plan that puts at least one day somewhere other than Uptown. Our Sedona in November guide covers the rest of the month if you have flexibility.

Sedona Thanksgiving dinner 2026: where to book

Several Sedona resorts publish their 2026 Thanksgiving menus in early November, and the best seats go first. Two that have posted details:

L’Auberge de Sedona (Cress on Oak Creek). According to L’Auberge’s Thanksgiving page, there are two ways to do the holiday on Thursday, November 26: a daytime Thanksgiving buffet (listed at $150 per adult and $75 per child) and a family-style Thanksgiving dinner in the evening ($140 per adult, $50 per child). The resort’s events calendar shows the buffet from 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. and dinner from 5 to 9 p.m. Reservations are highly recommended. This is the creekside setting under the sycamores, and it is about as classic a Sedona Thanksgiving as it gets.

Enchantment Resort (Boynton Canyon). Enchantment’s 2026 Thanksgiving page lists a Thanksgiving Day buffet in the Agave Ballroom from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., open to resort guests and the public, with reservations required. The resort also lists a morning Turkey Trot at 9 a.m. from the Trail House (donation based, up to five miles, with proceeds to Verde Valley Sanctuary), a complimentary Thanksgiving gathering circle at 10 a.m., and a Turkey Burner Vinyasa Flow class on Friday, November 27. Some of these ask for a reservation 24 hours ahead, so call the resort to confirm who can join.

Beyond the resorts, many of Sedona’s independent restaurants serve a holiday menu or a turkey special, while others close so their staff can have the day off. Every year the details change, so check each restaurant’s own site or call this week. If you would rather cook, book a rental with a real kitchen and shop early; grocery stores in Sedona and Cottonwood are busiest on Tuesday and Wednesday of Thanksgiving week.

A few tips from years of watching visitors scramble:

  • Book now, not next week. Resort Thanksgiving seatings in Sedona regularly sell out before the holiday.
  • Go early or late. A noon buffet frees up your evening for a sunset walk; a 5 p.m. dinner gives you the whole day outside.
  • Pick a view on purpose. Sunset is around 5:15 p.m. in late November, so an early dinner seating can catch the red rocks glowing.

For more places to eat on the other days of your trip, see our guide to Sedona restaurants with a view.

What’s open (and closed) on Thanksgiving Day

Thursday is the one day of the week when a lot of the usual plan changes. What we have verified for 2026:

  • Slide Rock State Park keeps short holiday hours on Thanksgiving, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m., per Arizona State Parks. The swim area is not the draw this time of year, but the creekside walk and orchard are lovely in the cold sun.
  • Montezuma Castle National Monument is closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas, according to the National Park Service. Save it for Friday.
  • Lowell Observatory in Flagstaff is closed on Thanksgiving Day, so plan stargazing elsewhere that night.
  • Trails and scenic drives are open as always. Thanksgiving morning is one of the quieter windows of the whole week for popular trailheads, because so many visitors are cooking or sleeping in. If you want a hike, this is the morning to do it.
  • Shops and galleries. Many Uptown and Tlaquepaque shops close or shorten hours on Thanksgiving Day, then open for the Black Friday weekend. Check the shop’s own hours if one is on your list.

Black Friday and the long weekend: wine tasting and holiday kickoffs

The Friday through Sunday after Thanksgiving is when Sedona wine country shines. Tasting rooms in Old Town Cottonwood, Page Springs and Cornville are busy but relaxed, and the cool afternoons are perfect for reds. Page Springs Cellars states that it is open 365 days a year, which makes it a safe bet even on the holiday itself; most other tasting rooms set their own holiday hours, so check before you go. Our list of wineries open Sunday and Monday is a good starting point for the weekend, and our Sedona wineries and vineyards guide lays out the three wine areas.

If wine tasting is part of your long weekend, this is the weekend not to drive. Thanksgiving traffic on 89A and the Page Springs roads is heavy, parking at the popular rooms is tight, and nobody in your group should have to sit out the pours. Holiday-weekend tour dates fill quickly, so call us at (928) 504-2445 to ask what is still open, or start with our Wine Tours of Sedona page to see how a day works.

The holiday season also gets going right after Thanksgiving:

  • Enchantment’s Red Rock Holidays program lists complimentary skating daily from November 28 through January 31, 3 to 7 p.m., plus fireside wine and cocktail experiences. Details and any guest-only restrictions are on Enchantment’s Red Rock Holidays page.
  • The Tlaquepaque tree lighting and Santa visit is the following Friday, December 4, 2026, from 5 to 6:30 p.m. at Tlaquepaque North, and admission is free, per Visit Sedona. If you are staying through the first weekend of December, it is worth the stop.

A four-day Thanksgiving weekend in Sedona

Here is how we would spend the long weekend, built around beating the crowds:

Wednesday. Arrive by mid-afternoon if you can, before the evening rush on I-17 and 89A. Pick up groceries, then watch sunset from Airport Mesa.

Thursday (Thanksgiving). Early hike while the trailheads are quiet, then a creekside lunch or Thanksgiving buffet. An afternoon at Slide Rock (open until 2 p.m.) or a slow scenic drive up Oak Creek Canyon. Evening dinner, or a cozy night in with a bottle of Arizona wine.

Friday. Montezuma Castle in the morning (it reopens after the holiday), then wine tasting in Page Springs or Old Town Cottonwood in the afternoon. Skip Uptown on Black Friday afternoon unless shopping is the point.

Saturday. Head up 89A for a day in Jerome and Cottonwood, or spend it in Uptown and Tlaquepaque early in the morning before the crowds arrive. Our Jerome wine tasting guide pairs well with this day.

Sunday. Leave early. The drive back toward Phoenix on Sunday afternoon of Thanksgiving weekend is one of the slowest of the year.

What to book, what to bring, when to go

What to book now: lodging (especially Wednesday through Saturday nights), your Thanksgiving dinner seating, any wine tasting or guided day, and dinner reservations for Friday and Saturday too.

What to bring: layers. A warm jacket, hat and gloves for mornings and evenings, sun protection for the afternoons, and good shoes, since even “easy” Sedona trails are rocky. If you plan to hike, carry water; dry air dehydrates you quickly even when it is cool.

When to go: if your dates are flexible, the week before Thanksgiving gives you the same weather and fall color with far fewer people. If Thanksgiving week is your week, embrace it: start early, eat early, and keep one day for the quieter Verde Valley.
